13. Wiring (EI&C)

Cabinets and junction boxes in their locations, rails, terminal strips and terminals, I/O cards and channels, cables and their cores landed on terminals, tracing a circuit from the field to the I/O channel, construction status, and cable lengths.

10 features7 availableGuide updated 2026-11-11

About this guide

In E1 wiring is data, not a drawing. A cabinet or junction box has a location and an enclosure; the enclosure holds rails, terminal strips and I/O cards; cables have their cores, and each core is landed on a terminal at both ends. From that data E1 traces every circuit from the field device to its I/O channel.

Typical workflow

  1. Make the locations and enclosures, with their rails, strips and cards.
  2. Add terminals to the strips, the channels and the instruments.
  3. Make the cables from the catalog's cable types, and land their cores on the terminals.
  4. Trace a circuit to check it reaches its channel, and set its construction status as the work goes on.
  5. Keep the cable lengths from 3D and by hand, and see where they differ; import the wiring of an existing plant or a vendor panel.
  6. See what is coming: loop diagrams from the wiring, wiring typicals and allocation in Panorama EI&C.

Before you start

You have a project whose library includes the Electrical foundation package (E1 library version 17 or newer: see Project settings). The examples use a marshalling cabinet A3 in equipment room CR1, a junction box JB-12, a transmitter TT-101, a field cable W-101 and a multicore W-201; on a project of your own, use your own names.

Cabinets and junction boxes: where they are and what they hold

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-enclosuresRoadmap 28.1Review check R1

A cabinet or junction box is two objects. Its location (an equipment room or a field location) is in Areas; the enclosure (a junction box, marshalling cabinet, system cabinet, local panel or vendor panel) is in Assets and is located in it. The enclosure holds rails, terminal strips, I/O cards with their channels, interposing devices (barriers, isolators, relays), power supplies, earth bars and cable entries. An MCC and a distribution board are enclosures too.

Where
Right-click Areas → New area…; right-click Assets → New asset…; right-click an enclosure, rail, I/O card or interposing device → New part…; the object window's Relationships tab
Who
A license and a project role that may edit objects
Before you start
A project is expanded in the explorer.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click Areas → New area…, click Equipment room, type CR1 in Tag and click Create.CR1 opens in its object window and is listed under Areas.
2Right-click Assets → New asset…, search marshalling, click Marshalling cabinet, type A3 in Tag and click Create.A3 opens. On its Attributes tab, Function is Marshalling; the group Size has width, height and depth, and Heat its heat limit.
3On A3's Relationships tab choose is located in, click CR1 in the tree and click Add.A group "is located in" holds CR1. An enclosure is located in one place.
4In the explorer, right-click A3 → New part… and click Rail; type R1 in Name and click Create.The window New object in A3 offers only what an enclosure may hold: rail, terminal strip, I/O card, interposing device, power supply, earth bar, cable entry and wires. R1 is listed under A3.
5Right-click R1 → New part…, click Terminal strip, type X1 in Name and click Create.X1 is listed under R1. Its Role can be set to Field side, System side, Power, Earth or Spare.
6Right-click A3 → New part…, click I/O card, choose AI card 16 channels, HART in From catalog, type A5 in Name and click Create.A5 takes the card's I/O kind and heat losses from the catalog. Right-click A5 → New part… → Channel makes its channels.
7Make a junction box JB-12 in a field location the same way, with a strip X1.The enclosures, their rails, strips and cards are a tree in Assets.

Two strips of one enclosure cannot have the same name, and neither can two rails or cards. A strip may hang on a rail or directly on the enclosure.

Add terminals to a strip

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-terminalsRoadmap 28.1Review check R1

A terminal strip gets its terminals in one step: you say how many, and E1 numbers them on from the last one. The same works for a channel of an I/O card or a barrier, an earth bar, a power supply, and an instrument's own terminals.

Where
Right-click a terminal strip, channel, earth bar or power supply in the explorer → Add terminals…
Who
A license and a project role that may edit objects
Before you start
The enclosure and its strip exist (Cabinets and junction boxes).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click strip X1 of A3 → Add terminals….E1 asks "How many terminals? Add a first number after a space to count from it (for example "12" or "12 21")."
2Type 12 and press Enter.X1 has terminals 1 to 12, in order, under it in the explorer.
3Right-click X1 → Add terminals… again and type 4.Terminals 13 to 16 are added after the last.
4Right-click X1 → Add terminals… and type 2 5.Refused: "The strip has terminals 5, 6 already."
5Right-click a channel of card A5 → Add terminals… and type 2.The channel has terminals 1 and 2.

Each terminal has a kind (feed-through, fused, disconnect, test, earth, multi-level), a width, a construction status and a scope on its Attributes tab. Asked through Alex, terminals can also take a terminal type from the catalog and end stops before and after.

An instrument's own terminals are added through Alex for now: its right-click menu has no Add terminals… yet.

Cables, their cores, and landing a core on a terminal

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-cables-coresRoadmap 28.1Review check R2

A cable made from a cable type in the catalog gets its cores at once: for a cable of pairs, each pair's two cores (P1-1, P1-2), the pair's screen (P1-S) when the pairs are screened, and the overall screen (OS). Each core is landed on a terminal at each end, never more.

Where
Right-click Assets → New asset… → Cable with From catalog; right-click a cable → Create cores; right-click a core or wire → Connect to terminal…
Who
A license and a project role that may edit objects and relationships
Before you start
The strips have their terminals (Add terminals).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click Assets → New asset…, click Cable, choose Multicore 12 pairs 0.75 mm², individual and overall screens in From catalog, type W-201 in Tag and click Create.W-201 opens. Expanded in the explorer, it has 37 cores: P1-1, P1-2, P1-S … P12-S and OS. Each core's Role is Conductor or Screen.
2Make a cable without a catalog item, set Core grouping to Pairs and Groups to 2, then right-click it → Create cores.The cable has P1-1, P1-2, P2-1 and P2-2. A second Create cores is refused: "W-… has 4 cores already."
3Right-click core P1-1 of W-201 → Connect to terminal…, type JB-12/X1/1 and press Enter.The core is landed on terminal 1 of strip X1 in JB-12. A path names the enclosure (or instrument) by its tag, then the strip and the terminal; a rail between them may be left out.
4Connect P1-1 again to A3/X1/1.The core is now landed at both ends.
5Connect P1-1 a third time, to A3/X1/3.Refused: "P1-1 is landed at both ends already."

A wire owned by a cabinet, with no cable, is a cross-wire between the field side and the system side. A jumper joins two terminals of one strip. Alex can land many cores in one step.

Trace a circuit

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-traceRoadmap 28.1Review check R2

E1 follows a circuit through cores, wires and jumpers, through feed-through terminals and across barriers and isolators, to the I/O channel, the power supply and the earth bar. It names the places on the way and every terminal by its reference designation, for example +CR1-A3-X1:5.

Where
Right-click an enclosure, strip, channel, terminal, cable or core in the explorer → Trace circuit
Who
Anyone who may view the project
Before you start
The cores are landed (Cables and their cores).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click field cable W-101 → Trace circuit.E1 shows the way, for example "TT-101 → JB-12 → A3", and where it ends: "Ends at +CR1-A3-1:1 (channel), +CR1-A3-1:2 (channel)."
2Land one more core of W-201 at JB-12 only, and trace W-201.The message adds "1 line landed at one end only."
3Trace a strip with nothing landed on it."It reaches no channel yet."

The designation is built from where things are: + the location's tag, - the enclosure's tag and the strip's (or card's channel's) name, : the terminal. Alex answers "where does TT-101 land?" with the same trace.

Construction status of cores and terminals

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-construction-statusRoadmap 28.1Review check R2

Every landed core and every terminal has a construction status: Designed, Issued for construction, Installed, Terminated, Loop-checked, As-built, or To be removed. A core's status is kept at both of its ends.

Where
Right-click a cable, core, wire or terminal in the explorer → Construction status ▸
Who
A license and a project role that may edit relationships
Before you start
The cores are landed.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click cable W-101 → Construction status ▸ Installed.Every landed core of W-101 is Installed at both ends. One Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) takes it back.
2Right-click a terminal → Construction status ▸ Terminated.The terminal's Construction status attribute is Terminated.
3Right-click a core that is not landed → Construction status ▸ Installed.Refused: "Nothing to change: the lines have no connections yet."

A connection can also be marked fixed (allocation will never move it) and given a scope (project, vendor, client, existing), through Alex for now.

Cable lengths and the wiring settings

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-cable-lengthsRoadmap 28.1Review check R3

A cable has a Length (by hand) and a Length from 3D (written by the 3D model's script through E1's API). E1 fills Length used itself: the length by hand when there is one, else the one from 3D. When the two differ by more than the project's tolerance, that is a finding.

Where
A cable's Attributes tab, group Line; Configuration → Project settings → Wiring (also File → Project settings…)
Who
Lengths: a license and a project role that may edit objects. Settings: a project role that may configure the project.
Before you start
A cable exists.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1On W-201's Attributes tab, type 120 m in Length from 3D.Length used shows 120 m.
2Type 100 m in Length (by hand).Length used shows 100 m: the length by hand wins.
3Try to type in Length used.Refused: "The length used is calculated from the length by hand and the length from 3D; set one of those."
4Open Configuration → Project settings and go to the Wiring card.Cable length tolerance is 10 %, Name printed first is ISA tag (TT-101), and Print the other name beside it is ticked.
5Type 25 in Cable length tolerance and leave the box.Saved at once. With 100 m by hand and 120 m from 3D the lengths now agree; at 10 % they did not.

The wiring check (ask Alex to "check the wiring of W-201") lists lengths that differ, conductor cores landed at one end only, a terminal carrying the cores of two cables, and a strip longer than its rail.

The findings of the wiring check are not yet in a panel of their own; Alex reports them.

Related: Cables and their cores · Project settings

Import existing or vendor wiring

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-importRoadmap 28.1Review check R3

The wiring of an existing plant, or of a vendor's panel, comes in as a termination list: one row per core with its two ends. E1 shows first what it would do, then makes the enclosures, strips, terminals, cables and cores not yet in the project and lands every core as fixed, so the new design is made around it and never moves it.

Where
Alex in the side panel; there is no button on the screen for it yet
Who
A license and a project role that may edit objects
Before you start
A CSV or Excel file with the columns From, From strip, From terminal, Cable, Core, Wire number, To, To strip, To terminal, and optionally From class and To class for enclosures E1 does not have yet (for example Marshalling cabinet; without it, a Junction box).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Attach the file in the side panel and ask Alex: "Import this termination list as existing wiring, preview first."Alex says how many rows it would create, how many are unchanged and which have a problem, with the row number and why (for example "To: name the strip of CAB-9"). Nothing is changed yet.
2Ask Alex to import it.The enclosures, strips, terminals, the cables and their cores appear in Assets. Every core is landed at both ends, fixed, with the scope Existing and the construction status As-built (Designed for a vendor's list unless you name another). A row with a problem is left out. One Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) takes the whole import back.
3Ask Alex to import the same list again.Nothing changes: every row is unchanged.
4Change one row so a core lands on another terminal, and import it with a preview.That row is a problem: the core "is landed elsewhere on a fixed connection: the list differs from the project". E1 never moves a fixed core by itself.

Loop diagrams from the wiring

PlannedWIRE-loop-diagramRoadmap 28.2

The loop diagram will follow each circuit of a loop through the junction boxes, cables, marshalling and barriers to the I/O channel, with its power feed and its screen, in columns by zone. Loop diagrams of a unit will be generated and issued as one set.

Today's loop diagram shows the loop's devices and the lines between them: see Loop diagram.

Signals and wiring typicals

PlannedWIRE-typicalsRoadmap 28.3

Devices will get their signals, and a wiring typical with its options and variants (for example "AI 2-wire, intrinsically safe through a barrier") that says which cables, cores, terminals and channel each signal needs, with names from the tag rules.

Related: Allocation and Panorama EI&C

Allocation and Panorama EI&C

PlannedWIRE-allocationRoadmap 28.4

E1 will propose where every signal lands, keeping the segregation rules, redundancy and spares, and you will walk the proposal hop by hop in Panorama EI&C, from the field to the I/O channel, and accept it.
