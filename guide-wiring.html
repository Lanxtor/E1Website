13. Wiring (EI&C)

Cabinets and junction boxes in their locations, rails, terminal strips and terminals, I/O cards and channels, cables and their cores landed on terminals, tracing a circuit from the field to the I/O channel, construction status, and cable lengths.

17 features17 availableGuide updated 2026-11-11

About this guide

In E1 wiring is data, not a drawing. A cabinet or junction box has a location and an enclosure; the enclosure holds rails, terminal strips and I/O cards; cables have their cores, and each core is landed on a terminal at both ends. From that data E1 traces every circuit from the field device to its I/O channel.

Typical workflow

  1. Make the locations and enclosures, with their rails, strips and cards.
  2. Add terminals to the strips, the channels and the instruments.
  3. Make the cables from the catalog's cable types, and land their cores on the terminals.
  4. Trace a circuit to check it reaches its channel, and set its construction status as the work goes on.
  5. Keep the cable lengths from 3D and by hand, and see where they differ; import the wiring of an existing plant or a vendor panel.
  6. Generate the loop diagrams from the wiring, issue them as a set, and build the wire list and other lists as queries.
  7. Give devices a wiring typical, let E1 propose where their signals land and accept it in Panorama EI&C, check the entity and voltage budget, and after issue work with the wire move list.
  8. See each cabinet's fill and heat balance, exchange the I/O list with the control-system vendor, and export markers.

Before you start

You have a project whose library includes the Electrical foundation package (E1 library version 17 or newer: see Project settings). The examples use a marshalling cabinet A3 in equipment room CR1, a junction box JB-12, a transmitter TT-101, a field cable W-101 and a multicore W-201; on a project of your own, use your own names.

Cabinets and junction boxes: where they are and what they hold

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-enclosuresRoadmap 28.1Review check R1

A cabinet or junction box is two objects. Its location (an equipment room or a field location) is in Areas; the enclosure (a junction box, marshalling cabinet, system cabinet, local panel or vendor panel) is in Assets and is located in it. The enclosure holds rails, terminal strips, I/O cards with their channels, interposing devices (barriers, isolators, relays), power supplies, earth bars and cable entries. An MCC and a distribution board are enclosures too.

Where
Right-click Areas → New area…; right-click Assets → New asset…; right-click an enclosure, rail, I/O card or interposing device → New part…; the object window's Relationships tab
Who
A license and a project role that may edit objects
Before you start
A project is expanded in the explorer.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click Areas → New area…, click Equipment room, type CR1 in Tag and click Create.CR1 opens in its object window and is listed under Areas.
2Right-click Assets → New asset…, search marshalling, click Marshalling cabinet, type A3 in Tag and click Create.A3 opens. On its Attributes tab, Function is Marshalling; the group Size has width, height and depth, and Heat its heat limit.
3On A3's Relationships tab choose is located in, click CR1 in the tree and click Add.A group "is located in" holds CR1. An enclosure is located in one place.
4In the explorer, right-click A3 → New part… and click Rail; type R1 in Name and click Create.The window New object in A3 offers only what an enclosure may hold: rail, terminal strip, I/O card, interposing device, power supply, earth bar, cable entry and wires. R1 is listed under A3.
5Right-click R1 → New part…, click Terminal strip, type X1 in Name and click Create.X1 is listed under R1. Its Role can be set to Field side, System side, Power, Earth or Spare.
6Right-click A3 → New part…, click I/O card, choose AI card 16 channels, HART in From catalog, type A5 in Name and click Create.A5 takes the card's I/O kind and heat losses from the catalog. Right-click A5 → New part… → Channel makes its channels.
7Make a junction box JB-12 in a field location the same way, with a strip X1.The enclosures, their rails, strips and cards are a tree in Assets.

Two strips of one enclosure cannot have the same name, and neither can two rails or cards. A strip may hang on a rail or directly on the enclosure.

Add terminals to a strip

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-terminalsRoadmap 28.1Review check R1

A terminal strip gets its terminals in one step: you say how many, and E1 numbers them on from the last one. The same works for a channel of an I/O card or a barrier, an earth bar, a power supply, and an instrument's own terminals.

Where
Right-click a terminal strip, channel, earth bar or power supply in the explorer → Add terminals…
Who
A license and a project role that may edit objects
Before you start
The enclosure and its strip exist (Cabinets and junction boxes).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click strip X1 of A3 → Add terminals….E1 asks "How many terminals? Add a first number after a space to count from it (for example "12" or "12 21")."
2Type 12 and press Enter.X1 has terminals 1 to 12, in order, under it in the explorer.
3Right-click X1 → Add terminals… again and type 4.Terminals 13 to 16 are added after the last.
4Right-click X1 → Add terminals… and type 2 5.Refused: "The strip has terminals 5, 6 already."
5Right-click a channel of card A5 → Add terminals… and type 2.The channel has terminals 1 and 2.

Each terminal has a kind (feed-through, fused, disconnect, test, earth, multi-level), a width, a construction status and a scope on its Attributes tab. Asked through Alex, terminals can also take a terminal type from the catalog and end stops before and after.

An instrument's own terminals are added through Alex for now: its right-click menu has no Add terminals… yet.

Cables, their cores, and landing a core on a terminal

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-cables-coresRoadmap 28.1Review check R2

A cable made from a cable type in the catalog gets its cores at once: for a cable of pairs, each pair's two cores (P1-1, P1-2), the pair's screen (P1-S) when the pairs are screened, and the overall screen (OS). Each core is landed on a terminal at each end, never more.

Where
Right-click Assets → New asset… → Cable with From catalog; right-click a cable → Create cores; right-click a core or wire → Connect to terminal…
Who
A license and a project role that may edit objects and relationships
Before you start
The strips have their terminals (Add terminals).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click Assets → New asset…, click Cable, choose Multicore 12 pairs 0.75 mm², individual and overall screens in From catalog, type W-201 in Tag and click Create.W-201 opens. Expanded in the explorer, it has 37 cores: P1-1, P1-2, P1-S … P12-S and OS. Each core's Role is Conductor or Screen.
2Make a cable without a catalog item, set Core grouping to Pairs and Groups to 2, then right-click it → Create cores.The cable has P1-1, P1-2, P2-1 and P2-2. A second Create cores is refused: "W-… has 4 cores already."
3Right-click core P1-1 of W-201 → Connect to terminal…, type JB-12/X1/1 and press Enter.The core is landed on terminal 1 of strip X1 in JB-12. A path names the enclosure (or instrument) by its tag, then the strip and the terminal; a rail between them may be left out.
4Connect P1-1 again to A3/X1/1.The core is now landed at both ends.
5Connect P1-1 a third time, to A3/X1/3.Refused: "P1-1 is landed at both ends already."

A wire owned by a cabinet, with no cable, is a cross-wire between the field side and the system side. A jumper joins two terminals of one strip. Alex can land many cores in one step.

Trace a circuit

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-traceRoadmap 28.1Review check R2

E1 follows a circuit through cores, wires and jumpers, through feed-through terminals and across barriers and isolators, to the I/O channel, the power supply and the earth bar. It names the places on the way and every terminal by its reference designation, for example +CR1-A3-X1:5.

Where
Right-click an enclosure, strip, channel, terminal, cable or core in the explorer → Trace circuit
Who
Anyone who may view the project
Before you start
The cores are landed (Cables and their cores).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click field cable W-101 → Trace circuit.E1 shows the way, for example "TT-101 → JB-12 → A3", and where it ends: "Ends at +CR1-A3-A5-1:1 (channel), +CR1-A3-A5-1:2 (channel)."
2Land one more core of W-201 at JB-12 only, and trace W-201.The message adds "1 line landed at one end only."
3Trace a strip with nothing landed on it."It reaches no channel yet."

The designation is built from where things are: + the location's tag, - the enclosure's tag and the strip's (or card's channel's) name, : the terminal. Alex answers "where does TT-101 land?" with the same trace.

Construction status of cores and terminals

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-construction-statusRoadmap 28.1Review check R2

Every landed core and every terminal has a construction status: Designed, Issued for construction, Installed, Terminated, Loop-checked, As-built, or To be removed. A core's status is kept at both of its ends.

Where
Right-click a cable, core, wire or terminal in the explorer → Construction status ▸
Who
A license and a project role that may edit relationships
Before you start
The cores are landed.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click cable W-101 → Construction status ▸ Installed.Every landed core of W-101 is Installed at both ends. One Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) takes it back.
2Right-click a terminal → Construction status ▸ Terminated.The terminal's Construction status attribute is Terminated.
3Right-click a core that is not landed → Construction status ▸ Installed.Refused: "Nothing to change: the lines have no connections yet."

A connection can also be marked fixed (allocation will never move it) and given a scope (project, vendor, client, existing), through Alex for now.

Cable lengths and the wiring settings

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-cable-lengthsRoadmap 28.1Review check R3

A cable has a Length (by hand) and a Length from 3D (written by the 3D model's script through E1's API). E1 fills Length used itself: the length by hand when there is one, else the one from 3D. When the two differ by more than the project's tolerance, that is a finding.

Where
A cable's Attributes tab, group Line; Configuration → Project settings → Wiring (also File → Project settings…)
Who
Lengths: a license and a project role that may edit objects. Settings: a project role that may configure the project.
Before you start
A cable exists.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1On W-201's Attributes tab, type 120 m in Length from 3D.Length used shows 120 m.
2Type 100 m in Length (by hand).Length used shows 100 m: the length by hand wins.
3Try to type in Length used.Refused: "The length used is calculated from the length by hand and the length from 3D; set one of those."
4Open Configuration → Project settings and go to the Wiring card.Cable length tolerance is 10 %, Name printed first is ISA tag (TT-101), and Print the other name beside it is ticked.
5Type 25 in Cable length tolerance and leave the box.Saved at once. With 100 m by hand and 120 m from 3D the lengths now agree; at 10 % they did not.

The wiring check (ask Alex to "check the wiring of W-201") lists lengths that differ, conductor cores landed at one end only, a terminal carrying the cores of two cables, and a strip longer than its rail.

The findings of the wiring check are not yet in a panel of their own; Alex reports them.

Import existing or vendor wiring

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-importRoadmap 28.1Review check R3

The wiring of an existing plant, or of a vendor's panel, comes in as a termination list: one row per core with its two ends. E1 shows first what it would do, then makes the enclosures, strips, terminals, cables and cores not yet in the project and lands every core as fixed, so the new design is made around it and never moves it.

Where
Alex in the side panel; there is no button on the screen for it yet
Who
A license and a project role that may edit objects
Before you start
A CSV or Excel file with the columns From, From strip, From terminal, Cable, Core, Wire number, To, To strip, To terminal, and optionally From class and To class for enclosures E1 does not have yet (for example Marshalling cabinet; without it, a Junction box).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Attach the file in the side panel and ask Alex: "Import this termination list as existing wiring, preview first."Alex says how many rows it would create, how many are unchanged and which have a problem, with the row number and why (for example "To: name the strip of CAB-9"). Nothing is changed yet.
2Ask Alex to import it.The enclosures, strips, terminals, the cables and their cores appear in Assets. Every core is landed at both ends, fixed, with the scope Existing and the construction status As-built (Designed for a vendor's list unless you name another). A row with a problem is left out. One Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) takes the whole import back.
3Ask Alex to import the same list again.Nothing changes: every row is unchanged.
4Change one row so a core lands on another terminal, and import it with a preview.That row is a problem: the core "is landed elsewhere on a fixed connection: the list differs from the project". E1 never moves a fixed core by itself.

Cabinet fill and heat balance

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-fill-heatRoadmap 28.5Review check R7

A cabinet's or junction box's page shows its fill: each strip as a bar of used, spare and free terminals, each card with its channels used by type. Below it, the cabinet's heat balance from physics: the heat its devices give off at their load (a card for the channels in use, a supply, a barrier, plus heat added by hand) against the heat its free walls, a fan and a cooling unit take away. The result is the inside temperature, the temperature at the top with the project's allowance, the margin to the cabinet's limit, and the cooling still missing.

Where
Open an enclosure (a cabinet, junction box, panel or MCC): Fill and heat; its Attributes tab, groups Size and Heat
Who
Anyone who may view the project; the values need a license and a role that may edit objects
Before you start
The enclosure has its width, height and depth, its Heat transfer coefficient (from the maker's data, about 5.5 W/(m²·K) for sheet steel), Ambient temperature and Heat limit; its devices have their heat loss (catalog items carry it).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1On a 600 × 2000 × 600 mm system cabinet S1, type back, left, bottom in Covered faces, 100 m³/h in Fan airflow, 50 W in Heat added by hand, a limit of 40 °C and an ambient of 35 °C.Each value is saved. The covered faces give no heat away: the free walls are the top, the front and the right, 2.76 m².
2Look at Fill and heat."36.7 °C inside, 41.7 °C at the top, 1.7 K above the limit; 83.8 W of cooling missing." Below it, how: the heat in, the walls and coefficient, the fan, and the devices that give the most heat.
3Add a power supply without a Heat loss.A finding: "S1: the heat loss of power supply G2 is not known; the heat balance does not count it."
4Type 100 W in Cooling power.The cabinet stays at the ambient temperature.

This is a heat balance from physics with every input shown; it is not a temperature-rise verification under IEC 61439 or IEC TR 60890, which a contract may still ask for. The top allowance is 5 K unless the project sets another.

Exchange the I/O list with the control-system vendor

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-io-listRoadmap 28.5Review check R7

The I/O list goes to the control-system vendor with every signal, its kind, segregation class, control system and HART, and the cabinet, card, channel and terminals its circuit reaches. The vendor often assigns channels themselves. When their list comes back, a channel they kept is fixed, a channel they changed is a finding, and you decide whether E1 moves the cross-wires to it.

Where
A cabinet's page → Fill and heat → Export I/O list; for a unit and for importing, Alex in the side panel
Who
Exporting: anyone who may view the project. Importing: a license and a project role that may edit relationships

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Click Export I/O list on cabinet S1.A CSV file "S1 I-O list.csv" downloads with the columns Tag, Signal, Kind, Description, Segregation, Control system, HART, Cabinet, Card, Channel, Terminals.
2Attach the vendor's list in the side panel and ask Alex to import it for S1, as a preview.A row with the same card and channel will be fixed. A changed row is a finding: "FT-1 pv: the vendor has card A5 channel 2, E1 has card A5 channel 1. Apply to move to it." A channel that is taken or of another kind says so.
3Ask Alex to apply it.The cross-wires of FT-1 move to channel 2, fixed and in vendor scope; the next export shows channel 2.

Fieldbus segments

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-fieldbusRoadmap 28.6Review check R8

A fieldbus segment is one Foundation Fieldbus H1 or Profibus PA network: a power conditioner, a trunk cable, device couplers with their spur ports, and the devices on the spurs. Its limits are data the project edits: the current budget, the supply voltage and the lowest voltage a device needs, the most devices and final elements, the longest total length and spur, and the ports kept spare. A device goes on a segment only while the segment keeps within all of them.

Where
Making a segment and placing devices: Alex in the side panel. The segment's page → Segment view
Who
A license and a project role that may create objects; anyone who may view the project sees the segment view
Before you start
A published segment typical (Wiring typicals); each device has a signal of kind FF or PA with its fieldbus current, and a spur cable with its length.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Ask Alex to make segment SEG-1 from the segment typical "FF H1 FISCO" with trunk cable W-TR1.SEG-1 in Assets with its limits, power conditioner PC1 and device coupler DC1 with its spur ports.
2Ask Alex to place FT-01 to FT-06 on segments, first as a preview.Five go on SEG-1, each on a free port (DC1/1 …). FT-06 is not placed, with the reason: "SEG-1: 120 mA, over the current budget of 100 mA".
3Make a second segment and place them again.FT-06 goes on SEG-2. Devices already placed stay where they are; two devices of one redundancy group never share a segment.
4Open SEG-1.The Segment view: devices, current, the voltage at the farthest device, trunk and spurs, the longest spur, free ports and final elements against their limits, the rules it breaks and what is missing.
5Generate the loop diagram of a loop with FT-01.The segment is drawn in its own column, with the spur from the device to it. The wiring workbench says "On segment SEG-1".

The voltage is worked out from the trunk's and spurs' resistance per length; it is a check for design, not a vendor's segment calculation.

Loop diagrams from the wiring

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-loop-diagramRoadmap 28.2Review check R4

The loop diagram follows each circuit of a loop from its devices through the junction boxes, the cables, the marshalling and the barriers to the I/O channel, with the barrier's power feed and the screens to their earth bar. Every terminal it uses is drawn in a column by zone, left to right: Junction box, Marshalling: field side, Interposing, Marshalling: system side, I/O, and Supply and earth. Nothing of it is drawn by hand: it is made again from the wiring every time it is opened.

Where
Right-click a function in Systems → Generate loop diagram, Generate loop diagrams or Create revisions for the loop diagrams…
Who
A license and a project role that may make documents; issuing needs a role that may issue revisions
Before you start
The loop's instruments implement the function and their cores are landed (Cables and their cores).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click TIC-101 in Systems → Generate loop diagram.The loop diagram opens. The line above it reads, for example, "1 devices, 4 terminals, 4 lines, in signal order: field, junction box, marshalling, I/O." TT-101 is on the left; to its right the columns Junction box JB-12 and Marshalling: field side A3, each with the terminals the loop uses (JB-12 X1:1, JB-12 X1:2 …), and the cores drawn between them.
2Drag a terminal a little and click Regenerate.The terminal stays where you put it: "Moved by hand: …". A hand move is kept on the diagram, never in the wiring.
3In Configuration → Project settings → Wiring choose Name printed first → IEC 81346 reference designation (+CR1-A3-X1:5), then Regenerate.The terminals are named +CR1-A3-X1:1 and so on.
4Right-click the unit above several loops → Generate loop diagrams."3 loop diagrams generated." (or how many): every function below it that has devices has its loop diagram in Documents.
5Right-click the unit → Create revisions for the loop diagrams…, purpose IFC, keep the description.Each loop diagram gets its next revision, with one sign-off for the set: "3 revisions are in sign-off: open a loop diagram to sign them." (Sign-off)

A device without terminals is drawn as before, joined to the others by the lines drawn on the P&ID (Loop diagram). A core landed at one end only is counted above the diagram: "One core is not connected at one end." A screen earthed at one end is not counted: its other end is insulated on purpose.

The diagram's columns come after all of the loop's devices, also its controller and valve. The PDF of a set is one file per diagram: a single PDF with a contents page is not made yet.

Signals and wiring typicals

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-typicalsRoadmap 28.3Review check R5

Most devices of a plant follow a handful of patterns: a 2-wire transmitter, a solenoid valve, a motor-operated valve. A wiring typical describes one pattern once: the device's signals (open command, closed limit, position …), the field cables and which pair each signal takes, options such as Position feedback, variants (named sets of option values) and the naming of cables and wires. Given to a device, it makes the device's I/O signals, its terminals, and its field cables with their cores landed on the device and numbered.

Where
Right-click an instrument, valve or motor in the explorer → Wiring typical…; making and publishing a typical, and giving one to many devices at once: Alex in the side panel
Who
A license and a project role that may edit objects; publishing a typical in the project's library needs a role that may configure the project
Before you start
The devices have tags: they name the cables and the wire numbers.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Ask Alex: "Make a wiring typical Motor-operated valve for the project: open, close and stop commands, open and closed limits, torque fault and local/remote on an 8-pair control cable, and the position (AI, HART) on its own 1-pair cable when the option Position feedback is on; a variant Without position. Publish it."Alex saves the typical as a draft, checks it (every signal on a cable, every option it names exists) and publishes it. A typical with a mistake is refused with what is wrong, for example "Signal pv is on no cable."
2Right-click valve XV-101 → Wiring typical…, type 1 for Motor-operated valve, and leave the variant empty."XV-101 follows Motor-operated valve: 8 signals, 16 terminals, cables W-XV-101-1, W-XV-101-2." Under XV-101 are its I/O signals (open, close, … zt) and terminals 1 to 16; the two cables are in Assets with their cores, each core landed on its terminal and carrying its wire number (XV-101-open-1 …). XV-101's Wiring group shows the typical, the variant and the options.
3Do the same with the variant Without position on XV-102.Only the control cable is made: Position feedback is off.
4Ask Alex: "Give XV-103 to XV-112 the Motor-operated valve typical."The ten valves get their signals, terminals and cables in one step, which one Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) takes back.
5Run Wiring typical… again on XV-101.Nothing new is made: what is there by name or tag is kept.

Each I/O signal has its kind (AI, AO, DI, DO …), its segregation class, control system and redundancy group, used by the allocation. A terminal can also be made part of a potential (a 24 V rail, its common, an earth) with the relationship is part of potential.

There is no editor on the screen for typicals yet: they are made and published through Alex. Setting typicals by rule (for example every transmitter in a hazardous area) comes with rules; the junction box, marshalling and channel each signal lands on come with the allocation.

Allocation and Panorama EI&C

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-allocationRoadmap 28.4Review check R6

E1 proposes where every signal of a group of devices lands, in three hops: its field cable on a junction box strip, a free pair of a multicore to the marshalling cabinet, and two cross-wires to a free channel of its kind. It never breaks a hard rule: the control system, the project's segregation matrix and each strip's segregation class, redundancy groups apart (2oo3 on three junction boxes and three cards), the channel's type and HART, fixed and reserved terminals, and the spare percentage. Every choice says why, and every junction box it did not take says which rule it breaks. The same devices give the same proposal every time.

Where
Open a function (a loop, a unit, a system) in Systems, or right-click it → Panorama EI&C: its page shows Panorama EI&C with the wiring workbench
Who
Proposing: anyone who may view the project. Accepting: a license and a project role that may edit relationships
Before you start

Entity check and voltage budget

AvailableSince 2026-11-11WIRE-circuit-checksRoadmap 28.4Review check R6

For an intrinsically safe loop E1 compares the barrier's Uo, Io and Po with the device's Ui, Ii and Pi, and its Co and Lo with the device's Ci and Li plus the cable's capacitance and inductance for its length. For a loop with a supply it works out the voltage left at the device at 20 mA: the supply, less the barrier's drop, the cable's loop resistance and the channel's input resistance. A value it needs and does not have is reported as missing, never taken as zero.

Where
Alex in the side panel: "Check the wiring of TX-90." The cable, strip and terminal findings of the whole project are also in the Checks panel.
Who
Anyone who may view the project
Before you start
The device has its entity parameters and Lowest working voltage (group Wiring), the barrier comes from the catalog, the power supply has its Output voltage.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Ask Alex to check the wiring of TX-90 before its cable has a length."The entity check of TX-90 cannot be made: missing the length of W-90." and the same for the voltage budget.
2Give W-90 a Length (by hand) of 300 m and check again.No finding: the barrier fits, and enough voltage is left.
3Change the length to 1000 m and the device's lowest working voltage to 16.5 V."The entity check of TX-90 through U1 fails: Co 107 nF is below Ci 5 nF plus the cable's 110 nF." and "TX-90 gets 14.04 V at 20 mA … below its 16.5 V."

This is the entity concept with the values of the products' data; it does not replace the intrinsic safety verification a project's rules may ask for.
