Database

The database decides how fast E1 is.

What to choose

  • DatabasePostgreSQL 16
  • On Microsoft AzureAzure Database for PostgreSQL – Flexible Server, General Purpose (D2ds_v6 for a trial, D4ds_v6 for up to 50 engineers)
  • On your own serversDedicated cores and local SSD or a SAN with the IOPS above
  • ConnectionsAt least 300 for E1: each E1 server opens 41
  • StandbyA copy in another availability zone that takes over by itself, for daily work
  • BackupsPoint-in-time recovery, kept for 35 days

What not to choose

  • Burstable or shared-core databases (Azure Burstable B-series and the like): once their CPU credits are spent they slow to a fraction of a core, and E1 takes seconds per click.
  • Small, slow disks: a 32 GB disk gives 120 IOPS, too few for engineers working at once.
  • A database far from the E1 server: another region or data centre adds its delay to every step of every click.
  • A database shared with other applications: their load becomes your engineers' waiting time.

Questions

Questions IT teams ask.

Can we start smaller and grow later?

Yes. Start with the row that fits the people working at once today. A managed database moves to a larger size with one setting and a restart of a few minutes, and the E1 servers add themselves under load. Only the trial size is not meant for daily work.

Why not a cheaper burstable database?

A burstable database is fast only while it has CPU credits. Engineers work for hours at a time, the credits run out, and the database drops to a fraction of one core: every click then waits seconds. The money saved is lost many times over in waiting engineers.

Do you check our sizes before we start?

Yes. Tell us how many engineers will work at once and how large your projects are, and we confirm the sizes or adjust them. For more than 200 engineers at once we size the installation from a load test. Write to sales.
