Signals

Signals from wiring typicals

In E1, a wiring typical describes one kind of device once, such as a 2-wire transmitter, a solenoid valve or a motor-operated valve, and gives every device that uses it its I/O signals, terminals and field cables.

A typical holds the device's signals (open command, closed limit, position and so on), its field cables and the pair each signal takes, options such as position feedback, variants, and the naming of cables and wires. Set it on many devices at once and E1 makes their signals and cables with cores landed on the device and numbered.

The Wiring page of a unit, loop, cabinet, cable or device shows every signal in one row with its whole path: field cable, junction box, multicore, marshalling and I/O channel. A dash is a place not reached yet. Trace circuit follows a circuit through cores, jumpers, barriers and isolators to the I/O channel, the power supply and the earth bar, and names every terminal by its reference designation, such as +CR1-A3-X1:5.

Allocation

Wiring allocation that keeps the rules

In E1, Propose wiring fills each signal's empty places: a junction box strip, a free multicore pair to the marshalling cabinet, and cross-wires to a free channel of its kind.

The proposal never breaks a hard rule. It keeps the control system, the project's segregation matrix and each strip's segregation class, keeps redundancy groups apart (a 2oo3 group on three junction boxes and three cards), matches the channel's type and HART, leaves fixed and reserved terminals alone, and keeps the spare percentage. Every choice says why, and every junction box it did not take says which rule it breaks. The same devices give the same proposal every time.

Nothing changes until you accept. You accept one cell, a row, a column or everything, and a hop is never accepted without the one before it, so no core is left landed at one end. When every card of the marshalling cabinet is full, the channel is proposed on a new card in the first free slot of a rack of the signal's control system.

I/O list

Racks, cards and the I/O list for the DCS or PLC vendor

In E1, a cabinet's Contents page shows each rack with its control system and every slot, the card in each slot with its channels, and the transmitter landed on each channel.

Drag a card onto a free slot to move it; its cross-wires follow. Export I/O list gives the control-system vendor every signal with its kind, segregation class, control system and HART, and the cabinet, rack, slot, card, channel and terminals its circuit reaches. Vendors often assign channels themselves. When their list comes back, Alex imports it as a preview: a channel they kept becomes fixed, and a channel they changed is a finding, where you decide whether E1 moves the cross-wires.

On a circuit diagram, an I/O card is drawn as a PLC box whose connection points are its channels' terminals, named as the I/O list names them. A contact or lamp drawn in line with one is wired to the channel at once, so the wire list and the I/O list show it.

  • Fieldbus segments for Foundation Fieldbus H1 and Profibus PA, with current budget, voltages, device counts and lengths as limits a device must keep within.
  • The entity check for intrinsically safe loops and the voltage budget at 20 mA, with missing values reported as missing, never as zero.
Safety

Cause and effect, safety instrumented functions and alarms

In E1, the cause and effect matrix of a unit or loop is filled like a spreadsheet, with causes and devices chosen from lists of the project's instruments, interlocks and devices.

Each cell is set with a click or a key (C Close, O Open, S Stop, R Start), with an optional delay. Causes that vote are grouped, such as 2 of 3, and E1 writes the narrative from the matrix: when two of PT-101A, PT-101B, PT-101C read high-high above 12 bar(g), close XV-101 after 5 s. The matrix exports to the Excel workbook engineers hand over and reads back with a preview, and it is compared with any version or revision of its document.

An interlock becomes a safety instrumented function with its required SIL. Given failure rates and the proof test interval, E1 shows the PFD with the simplified low-demand equations of IEC 61508-6, the hardware fault tolerance, and whether the achieved SIL meets the requirement. Alarms are rationalized from severity and time to respond, and E1 proposes the priority after EEMUA 191 and ISA-18.2. Each plant state, such as Start-up or Shutdown, shows which alarms it suppresses.

SCD

System control diagrams to NORSOK I-005

In E1, the NORSOK SCD content package draws system control diagrams with the function templates of NORSOK I-005, every terminal of the standard named on the block.

The package brings the function templates such as MA, CA, LB and SBV, the non-standard function, the elementary functions, references, and sequence diagrams with steps, transitions and actions. Every parameter of a function is an attribute, and every process shape that fits a P&ID fits a system control diagram too.

Check SCD… reports signals that join two outputs, an analogue output wired to a binary input, and other breaches of the standard's rules, with Show to open the place on the drawing. Function tags follow NORSOK Z-DP-002, for example 43LIC0001.

Questions

Questions engineers ask.

Does E1 program the PLC or DCS?

No. E1 prepares the engineering the control system is built from: signals, I/O allocation, the I/O list, cause and effect, safety functions and alarms. The I/O list goes to the control-system vendor and their list comes back into E1.

What happens when the vendor changes channel assignments?

When their I/O list is imported, a kept channel becomes fixed and a changed one is a finding. You decide whether E1 moves the cross-wires to the vendor's channel.

Is the wiring proposal repeatable?

Yes. The same devices give the same proposal every time, every choice explains why, and nothing is changed until you accept it.

Can I keep working on the cause and effect matrix in Excel?

Yes. Export it as a workbook with its legend and narratives, edit it, and import it back: E1 lists what it will add, change and remove before anything changes, and one Undo takes the import back.

Does the SIL calculation replace a SIL verification?

It gives the PFD, hardware fault tolerance and achieved SIL from the simplified equations of IEC 61508-6. Diagnostics, repair time and partial stroke tests are not counted, so follow your project's verification rules.

Is NORSOK SCD switched on in every project?

No. It is off in a new project. A project administrator switches it on in Project settings → Content packages, and it switches Plant engineering and Electrical foundation on with it.
