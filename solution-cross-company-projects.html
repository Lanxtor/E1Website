Sharing

Share a live project with another organization

In E1, a Project administrator shares a project with another organization, and that organization's administrator decides which of its own people work on it.

Owner-operator and EPC collaboration usually means copies: a model exported, zipped, emailed, changed and sent back, with nobody quite sure which file is current. In E1 there is one project. The client, the contractor and the specialists open the same objects, drawings, lists and documents, and see each other's changes within seconds.

Sharing takes two steps. In the project's Project settings → Access, the Project administrator chooses the highest role the other organization may give, for example at most Engineer, and clicks Share with another organization…. E1 shows a code once, valid for 14 days, to send to the other organization's administrator. There, under File → Organization… → Shared projects, the Owner or an Administrator enters the code, clicks Accept and gives each of their members a role on the project.

Their members then switch to the sharing organization and work on that project only. History shows their organization after their name, so every change says which company made it. Either side clicks End to stop: the other organization's people lose the project at once.

Boundaries

Clear boundaries between companies

In E1, the people of a partner organization are guests: they work within the role you allow and manage nothing of yours.

Whatever role a guest holds on the shared project, they manage nothing in your organization and have no integration access. The highest role you set when sharing caps what their administrator can give. The project roles are Viewer (views and exports), Reviewer (views and comments), Engineer (creates and edits objects, attributes, relations and drawings), Lead engineer (also issues revisions and approves branches) and Project administrator.

Inside the project, scopes are as clear as between disciplines. With Assign to…, a contractor's group becomes responsible for a section, a folder, a sheet or an object: only that group changes it, while everyone else views and comments. A responsibility never gives anyone a permission they do not have, and the same rule holds for Alex, imports and the API. Custom roles can limit a firm to one discipline's attributes, such as electrical or instrumentation.

A contractor's people use their own organization's licenses, not your seats. Guests invited to a project to view, such as clients and contractors, view for free.

Visibility

Decide what reviewers and viewers see

In E1, everything in a project is open to its members until you hide it: a whole project from viewers, or one attribute on a class or on one object.

Reviewers and viewers are often people outside your company: the client, a contractor, an inspector. A person with a Reviewer license is in the built-in group Reviewers, one with a Viewer license in Viewers, and you can tick anyone else into these groups, for example a contractor with an Editor license, so they see only what that group sees.

Set a project's Visible to so that viewers do not see it at all. Mark an attribute Editors only on a class, so that no reviewer or viewer sees, for example, the flow rate of any pump. Hide one value from viewers on one object. Hidden values are left out on the server, so they are missing everywhere for that circle: the object page, lists and their Excel exports, datasheets and their PDFs, history, Alex's context and the API.

Working together

One record instead of the latest ZIP

In E1, questions, tasks and decisions between companies are kept on the objects they are about.

Discuss an object on its Chat tab: @mention a colleague from either company, #link another object, reply and resolve. Give a person or a group a task with a type, a due date and a screenshot; they find it in their Inbox and mark it done. Keep PDFs, spreadsheets and drawings with an object or the project as attachments.

Deliverables between owner and contractor go through E1's document control: each revision is signed along its purpose's route and issued as a PDF that never changes, with what changed since the revision before (see revisions, datasheets and reports). Larger changes can be prepared in a branch and reviewed before they reach main (see branches).

Audit

Every step on the record

In E1, sharing a project, accepting it, giving roles and ending it are written to the audit logs of both organizations.

Owners and Administrators see every change to members, licenses, groups, roles and responsibilities with its before and after, see who had access on any earlier date, and export the history to Excel or CSV for an audit. An access review sends a project's access to a reviewer, who keeps or removes each line.

Where a company's privacy rules do not allow names, Configuration → Privacy makes history show only the groups people are in. E1 support has no access to your data. More on protection and data location is on the security page.

Questions

Questions engineers ask.

Who owns a project that is shared with another company?

The organization that shares it. The other organization's people work on it as guests, and the project stays with you when the sharing ends.

Does a contractor use our licenses?

No. The other organization's people use their own licenses, not your seats. Guests invited to view a project, such as clients and contractors, view for free.

How does the other company join the project?

Your Project administrator clicks Share with another organization… in the project's Access settings and sends the code, valid for 14 days. Their Owner or Administrator accepts it under File → Organization… → Shared projects and chooses their people and roles.

Can a contractor see everything in our project?

Only what you leave open. Hide a project from viewers, an attribute on a class from reviewers and viewers, or one value on one object. Hidden values are left out on the server, also in exports, PDFs, history and the API.

Can a contractor administer our organization?

No. Guests manage nothing and have no integration access, whatever role they hold on the project, and their roles are capped by the highest role you allowed.

How do we stop sharing?

Either organization clicks End. The other organization's people lose the project at once, and the step is in both audit logs.
