15. Cause and effect, safety functions, alarms

Interlocks with their causes and effects as the cause and effect matrix and its narratives, safety instrumented functions with their required SIL and PFD, alarms rationalized and suppressed by plant state, and the matrix and alarm list as documents.

7 features7 availableGuide updated 2026-11-11

About this guide

In E1 the cause and effect matrix, the safety instrumented functions and the alarm list are not spreadsheets beside the P&ID: they are part of the model. An interlock is a function in Systems; its causes are trips on the instruments that start it; its effects are what it does to valves, motors and equipment. Alarms watch instruments too. The matrix, the narratives, the PFD of each safety function and the alarm list are computed from them, so a renamed tag or a changed setting is right everywhere at once.

Typical workflow

  1. On a unit in Systems, fill its cause and effect matrix like a spreadsheet: type the tags of the causes, add the devices, set the cells, group the causes that vote, and read the narratives.
  2. Make the interlocks that protect against a hazard safety instrumented functions, give their failure rates and proof test interval, and check their PFD against the required SIL.
  3. Add the alarms, rationalize them, and choose which plant states suppress them.
  4. Export the matrix to Excel for the control system vendor and the test team, and import it back after it was edited there; make the matrix and the alarm list documents, issue them, and compare the matrix with the last revision.

Before you start

You have a project at E1 library version 28 or newer (Plant 1.16.0: see Project settings), a unit or loop in Systems, and its instruments, valves and motors in Assets. Cause and effect is part of Editor. The examples use a unit UNIT-100 with the pressure transmitters PT-101A, PT-101B, PT-101C, the level transmitter LT-102 and the shutdown valve XV-101.

The cause and effect matrix: causes, devices and cells

AvailableSince 2026-11-11SAFE-cause-effectRoadmap 34.1, 34.4Review check T1

On any unit or loop in Systems, fill the cause and effect matrix as you would in Excel: type the tags of the causes, add the devices as columns, set each cell with a click or a key, group the causes that vote, and read the narrative E1 writes from them.

Where
Right-click a unit or loop in Systems → Cause and effect, or open it and click Cause and effect in the tabs at the top of its window: the Cause and effect page, tab Matrix
Who
Editor, and a project role that may edit objects
Before you start
The unit and its instruments and devices exist.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click UNIT-100 → Cause and effect.The unit's window opens on its Cause and effect page, beside the unit's own page (the tab named UNIT-100), with the tabs Matrix, Safety functions and Alarms. It shows everything in the unit's part of the tree.
2In the last row, Type a tag to add a cause…: type PT-101A and pick it. Do the same for PT-101B and PT-101C.Three rows, each with its own interlock: I-PT-101A, I-PT-101B, I-PT-101C, condition High-high.
3In each row type the Setting 12 bar(g) and press Enter.The setting is saved; the narrative under the matrix says above 12 bar(g).
4Click + Device, type XV-101 and pick it.A column XV-101, its tag written upwards.
5Tick the three rows and choose Group ▾ → 2 of 3.One interlock I-PT-101A with the vote 2oo3: its tag, its vote and each of its cells span the three rows.
6Click I-PT-101A's cell under XV-101 and choose C Close; type 5 in Delay first to make it close 5 s later.The cell reads C (with 5 s under it).
7Click the interlock's tag, rename it I-101 and choose Reset Manual reset.The matrix and the narrative follow.
8Read Narrative under the matrix.I-101 (2oo3): when two of PT-101A, PT-101B, PT-101C read high-high above 12 bar(g), close XV-101 after 5 s. Manual reset.

Faster with the keyboard

  • A cell's letters: C Close, O Open, S Stop, R Start, D De-energize, E Energize, T Trip. Press the letter on a cell to set it; Space sets the device's usual action (a valve closes, a motor stops) or clears the cell; Delete clears it; Enter opens the choice.
  • The arrows move from cell to cell; the cell's row and column are highlighted. Shift+arrows select a block: a letter sets all of it, Ctrl+D fills its first row down.
  • Ctrl+C copies the selected cells, Ctrl+V pastes letters copied from Excel (such as C, S, C 5 s). A paste or a fill is one change: one Undo takes it back.
  • Delay in a row is how long the condition must last before the cause trips; a delay in a cell is how long after the trip the device acts. Bypass says the cause may be bypassed for maintenance, with the hours after which the bypass ends; without them it is a warning in Checks.
  • Type an interlock's tag in the last row to make a cause that is that interlock tripping: one matrix cascades into the next. An interlock that trips itself that way is an error.
  • Ungroup moves a ticked cause back into an interlock of its own, with a copy of the cells. Change the vote in the Vote cell.
  • The same instrument can start several interlocks at different settings: each row is its own trip.
  • An interlock without causes or effects, or with a vote higher than its causes, has a warning mark by its tag; its finding is listed under the narrative and in Checks.
  • The page on the plant's top function shows the whole plant. Alex reads and changes the same data: ask it for the matrix of a unit, or to add a cause.

Safety instrumented functions: required SIL, PFD and achieved SIL

AvailableSince 2026-11-11SAFE-sifRoadmap 34.2Review check T2

Make an interlock a safety instrumented function with its required SIL, give the failure rates and proof test interval, and see its PFD, achieved SIL and hardware fault tolerance.

Where
The Cause and effect panel, tab Safety functions; the safety function's and its causes' Attributes
Who
Editor, and a project role that may edit objects
Before you start
The interlock has its causes and effects.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1In the matrix click the interlock's tag and choose the Type Safety instrumented function and Required SIL SIL 2.In the matrix it shows SIL 2 beside its tag.
2Open the Safety functions tab.Its row names what is missing: the logic solver PFD, the λDU of each device.
3Open the safety function: give Proof test interval (12 month), Logic solver PFD (0.0001) and, if not 0.1, Common cause factor (β). Open each cause and give its λDU (1e-6 per hour); ask Alex to set the valve's λDU on the effect.The row shows sensors 2oo3 with HFT 1, final elements 1oo1 with HFT 0, PFD 5.0 × 10⁻³, achieved SIL SIL 2, Meets its SIL.
4Change the required SIL to SIL 3.The row says why it falls short (the PFD, and the hardware fault tolerance of the single valve); Checks lists both.
  • The PFD uses the simplified equations of IEC 61508-6 for low demand, per group of sensors and final elements, with the highest λDU of a group. Diagnostics, repair time and partial stroke tests are not counted.
  • The hardware fault tolerance a SIL asks for follows IEC 61511: 1 for SIL 3, 2 for SIL 4.
  • An instrument whose own Safety integrity level is lower than the safety function's is a warning.

Alarms: priority, rationalization and plant states

AvailableSince 2026-11-11SAFE-alarmsRoadmap 34.3Review check T3

Add alarms on instruments, rationalize them so E1 proposes their priority, and see which alarms each plant state shows or suppresses.

Where
The Cause and effect panel, tab Alarms
Who
Editor, and a project role that may edit objects
Before you start
The unit has its instruments.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Click Add alarm: LT-102, Alarm type Low, Alarm setting 20 %, and Add.The alarm LT-102 AL is listed. It is a function under the instrument's loop, or under the unit.
2Click Rationalize… on its row: the cause, the consequence, Severity Major, Time to respond 10 to 30 min, the operator action.Proposed priority: Medium. Save gives it that priority and marks it rationalized. Choose Priority set by hand only to override it.
3Choose Plant state Shutdown.Each alarm reads Shown or Suppressed in it; the counts by priority are above the table.
4Tick LT-102 AL suppressed in Shutdown.It reads Suppressed in Shutdown and is shown in every other state.
  • The proposed priority follows a fixed matrix of severity and time to respond (after EEMUA 191 and ISA-18.2): Critical, High, Medium, Low or Journal.
  • A high alarm at or above a trip of the same instrument (or a low one at or below) is a warning: the operator has no time to act.
  • An alarm without a priority is a warning, one not rationalized is a note, and a project with 10 alarms or more of which more than 20 % are critical or high gets a warning.

Plant states

AvailableSince 2026-11-11SAFE-plant-statesRoadmap 34.3Review check T4

Name the states the plant runs in (Start-up, Normal operation, Shutdown, Maintenance): each alarm says in which of them it is suppressed.

Where
File → Project settings… (or Configuration → Project settings), card Plant states
Who
Editor, and a project role that may change project settings

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Open Plant states.Start-up, Normal operation, Shutdown and Maintenance, in order.
2Type Shut down over Shutdown, click Add plant state and type Standby, then Save.Saved; 1 alarm follows the change. An alarm suppressed in Shutdown is now suppressed in Shut down.
3Click Remove beside a state and Save.The state leaves every alarm's list.

A project has 1 to 12 plant states, each named once, without commas. Select one project to change them.

The cause and effect matrix and the alarm list as documents

AvailableSince 2026-11-11SAFE-documentsRoadmap 34.1, 34.3Review check T4

Make the matrix or the alarm list of a unit a document in Revisions, export it to Excel or PDF, and issue it like any document.

Where
The Cause and effect page: Excel ▾ → Make document on the Matrix tab, Make document on the Alarms tab; the document in Revisions
Who
Editor, and a project role that may create documents

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1On the Matrix tab choose Excel ▾ → Make document.Cause and effect matrix UNIT-100 is made in Revisions and opens. Clicked again, it opens the one there is.
2Export it to Excel.The workbook of Export to Excel: the sheets Matrix, Legend, Narratives (with each safety function's PFD) and Test sheet.
3Export it to PDF.The matrix with its letters, then the narratives and the test sheet.
4Add a revision and issue it.Issued, with the interlocks, trips, instruments and devices it shows. While one of its interlocks has an open error (no effect), issuing is refused: fix it or accept it in Checks.
5On the Alarms tab click Make document and export it.Alarm list UNIT-100: one row per alarm with its instrument, type, setting, priority, rationalization, and a column per plant state (Shown or Suppressed).

The documents are generated from the model each time they are exported, so they never fall behind the matrix.

The matrix in Excel, and back

AvailableSince 2026-11-13SAFE-excelRoadmap 34.4

Export a unit's cause and effect matrix as the workbook engineers hand over, with its legend, the narratives and a cause-by-cause test sheet; edit it in Excel and import it back, seeing every change before it is made.

Where
The Cause and effect page, tab Matrix: Excel ▾
Who
Editor; importing needs a project role that may create and edit objects
Before you start
The unit has its causes and devices.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Choose Excel ▾ → Export to Excel.A workbook downloads. Sheet Matrix: the title block, then Interlock, Vote, Cause, Instrument, Service, Condition, Setting, Delay (s), Bypass and a column per device; each interlock's tag, vote and cells merged down its cause rows, the device tags written upwards, the header and the left columns frozen, set to print landscape on one page wide.
2Open the sheets Legend, Narratives and Test sheet.The letters and how to read the vote; one sentence per interlock; one row per cause: what to apply (with the other causes its vote needs), what must happen (each device and its delay), the bypass check, and Result, Tested by, Date and Remarks to fill in.
3In Excel change I-101's cell under XV-101 to O 2 s, add a row for a new interlock, and save.—
4Choose Excel ▾ → Import from Excel… and pick the workbook.E1 lists what it will add, change and remove, and the rows it cannot read (an unknown tag or letter). Nothing is changed yet.
5Click Import.The matrix shows the changes; one Undo takes the whole import back.
  • Interlocks are found by their tag and causes by their instrument; a cause or a cell of an interlock in the sheet that is no longer in the sheet is removed. Interlocks that are not in the sheet stay as they are.
  • A cell is a letter, a letter with a delay (C 5 s, C5), an action's name, or X for the device's usual action.
  • Importing the exported workbook unchanged changes nothing.

What changed since the last revision

AvailableSince 2026-11-13SAFE-compareRoadmap 34.4

Compare a unit's cause and effect matrix with a version or revision of its document: the cells and rows that changed since are marked on the matrix.

Where
The Cause and effect page, tab Matrix: Compare ▾
Who
Anyone who may view the unit; versions only for people who see versions
Before you start
The unit's cause and effect document has a version or revision (make it and issue it).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Change a cell and add a cause after the revision was issued.—
2Choose Compare ▾ and the revision.A line says how many changes there are. A new cell or row is outlined green, a changed cell amber (point at it to read what it was), and what was removed is listed in red under the matrix.
3Click Done.The marks go.
