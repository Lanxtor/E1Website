About this guide

This guide covers who may do what in E1: your organization and its members, invitations, licenses and seats, organization roles and the passkey they need, the members and roles of a project and of a single drawing, what a refusal tells you, exporting the whole organization, trials, and the access control that is being built now.

Typical workflow

  1. Add a passkey to your account so your Owner or Administrator role takes effect: Organization roles and the passkey rule.
  2. Open the organization: File → Organization….
  3. Invite colleagues: Invitations.
  4. Give each person a license and watch the seats: Licenses and seats.
  5. Give people a role on a project: Project members and roles, or on one drawing: Roles on a drawing.
  6. Group people: Groups. Check what happened: Audit.
  7. Take everything out: Export everything….

Before you start

  • All of this needs E1 with sign-in (the cloud app, or a server your company runs). Without sign-in, invitations answer Invitations need sign-in on the PostgreSQL store (E1_AUTH=oidc).
  • You are signed in: Sign in.
  • What you may do is always your role limited by your license: a role gives permissions, and the license caps them.

There are no screenshots of the Organization dialog yet: it only works on a server with sign-in, where the screenshots have not been taken.

Licenses and seats

AvailableSince 2026-11-03Updated 2026-11-11ORG-licenses-seatsRoadmap M2.4, PR1Review check J8

Each member has one license, which caps what any of their roles allow. Your organization has a number of seats per license. Owners and Administrators are told in the Inbox when a license is 80 % and 100 % used.

Where
File → Organization… → Members → the License list of a person
Who
Owner or Administrator with a passkey
Before you start
An organization with 5 Editor seats (a trial has these) and several members.

The licenses

  • Editor: no cap; everything your roles allow.
  • Reviewer: view, comment, approve branches, export, and use Alex’s AI help.
  • Viewer: view and export.
  • Student: like Editor, without Alex’s AI help and assistants.
  • Administrator: organization administration only.
  • No license: the person can only view.

The License lists offer only the licenses your organization has seats for (and Viewer, which is unlimited); a person keeps the license they hold even when it has no seats left. The list follows E1's license catalog, so a new kind of license appears here when it is added.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1With 3 of 5 Editor seats used, set a fourth member's License to Editor.The seats line reads Editor 4 of 5 (Editor Basic 4 of 5 in an Essentials organization, see the plan). Every Owner and Administrator gets an Inbox notice E1 counted the seats: 4 of 5 Editor seats are in use (80 %).
2Give the fifth Editor license.A new notice: 5 of 5 Editor seats are in use (100 %). Nobody else can get this license until seats are added or one is freed.
3Try to give a sixth Editor license.Refused: All 5 Editor seats are taken.
4Sign in as a member who is not an Owner or Administrator and open the Inbox.No seat notice.

Seat notices come in the app only, never by email. A license also counts when it is given through an accepted invitation.

Seats are checked three times: when you invite, when the invitation is accepted, and when you give a license in the License list. Accepting and giving are checked one at a time per license, so two people can never take the same last seat. When none is left: All N Editor seats are taken.

Reviewers and Viewers are the same in every plan. A trial is Professional, so the team sees every feature.

Organization roles and the passkey rule

AvailableSince 2026-11-05Updated 2026-11-08ORG-roles-passkey

Organization roles say what someone may do for the whole organization, such as managing members or the company library. The powerful roles take effect only once the person has added a passkey: until then an Owner or Administrator acts as a Member, and a Project administrator acts as a Lead engineer.

Where
Your roles: the initial at the bottom of the left rail → Account → card Your access. Passkeys: the same page, card Passkeys.
Who
Everyone, for their own account
Before you start
E1 with sign-in, on a device or browser that supports passkeys.

The organization roles

  • Owner and Administrator: members, licenses, groups, audit and export.
  • Library administrator: Edits and publishes the library, the catalog and the symbols, and keeps Alex's company knowledge.
  • Catalog maintainer: Edits and publishes the catalog.
  • Engineering standards owner, Member and Guest.

The Account page also says: An organization role does not edit inside a project: that takes a role on the project.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1As an Owner without a passkey, open Account and look at Your access.The role reads acts as Member until you add a passkey, with the note Your Owner, Administrator or Project administrator role takes effect once you add a passkey below.
2Choose File → Organization… and try to give a license.Refused. The text ends with Your Owner role needs a passkey: add one under Account → Passkeys (your initial at the bottom of the left rail).
3On the Account page, card Passkeys, type a name (placeholder Name, for example Work laptop) and click Add a passkey. Complete the browser's prompt.Passkey added. The list shows Added <date> · last used <date>. A cancelled prompt shows No passkey was added.
4Look at Your access again and repeat step 2.The role reads Owner without the note; giving the license now works.

Confirm it is you

For important actions E1 asks you to confirm it is you when your sign-in or last confirmation is older than 15 minutes: exporting the organization, giving or removing Owner, Administrator or Library administrator, removing a member who is Owner, Administrator or Library administrator, changing an organization policy, deleting a project, adding or removing a passkey, and exporting a restricted project. The dialog Confirm it is you offers Cancel, Sign in again and Use passkey (when you have one).

Your installation's administrator can turn the passkey rule off for an installation.

The Account page shows Your access and Passkeys only with sign-in; without sign-in it is a placeholder.

Who administers the organization

AvailableSince 2026-11-07Updated 2026-11-08ORG-administrators

Every organization has at least one Owner. The person who starts it (for a trial: the person who accepted LANXTOR's trial invitation) is its first Owner. That is a starting point, not a decision: the Owner makes the right people Owner or Administrator and can then step back to Member. When the person who started the trial cannot do it, LANXTOR sets the administrators the company names.

Where
File → Organization… → Members, the Role list of each person
Who
An Owner gives and takes the Owner role. An Owner or an Administrator makes someone Administrator or Member. You give only a role whose permissions you all hold yourself, and nobody raises their own role. You confirm it is you (passkey rule).
Before you start
The person is already a member (invite them first). You have added a passkey.

The three choices

  • Owner: everything an Administrator does, plus billing, the subscription, and giving or taking the Owner role. Keep at least two Owners so the organization never depends on one person.
  • Administrator: members, invitations, licenses, groups, the audit and the export.
  • Member: no organization administration; what they do in projects comes from their project roles.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1As an Owner, choose File → Organization….The Members tab lists everyone with a Role list.
2In the row of the person who should run the organization, choose Owner in the Role list.When your sign-in is older than 15 minutes, Confirm it is you opens first. The list then shows Owner.
3In your own row, choose Member.You are a Member now and the Organization dialog is no longer yours to manage. When you were the only Owner, this is refused: An organization always keeps at least one Owner: make someone else Owner first.
4As an Administrator, choose Owner for someone.Refused: only an Owner gives the Owner role.
5As an Administrator, click Remove in the row of an Owner.Refused: only an Owner removes an Owner. An Owner who removes an Owner may first be asked to confirm it is you.
6Open the Audit tab.Each change is listed as access.org-role-set, with who made it.

A new Owner or Administrator acts as a Member until they add a passkey (passkey rule).

An organization role can be given only by someone who holds all of its permissions. An Administrator therefore cannot give Library administrator: You may not give Library administrator: it includes …, which you do not hold yourself. Nobody gives themselves an organization role, also not through a group, and nobody raises their own: You may not give yourself an organization role: another Owner or Administrator does that. or You may not raise your own organization role: another Owner does that.

When the person who started the trial is not the right administrator and is not available, the company writes to LANXTOR (sales@lanxtor.com) naming who it should be. LANXTOR sets it; the change shows in your Audit tab as done by LANXTOR support.

Colleagues with your company's email address can also ask to join by themselves, once LANXTOR has verified your domain: see Requests to join.

Project members and roles

AvailableSince 2026-11-03Updated 2026-11-13ORG-project-membersRoadmap M2.5, 09.6aReview check J3, J10

A role on a project decides what a person may do in it. The Project administrator admits people or groups to the project with a maximum role (the most they may hold anywhere in it) and gives them roles, in the project's Members or in Project settings → Access. Whoever creates a project becomes its Project administrator.

Where
Select the project in the explorer; on the project's page click Members. The same is in File → Project settings… → Access.
Who
Project administrator (with a passkey) gives and removes roles
Before you start
E1 with sign-in; a colleague in your organization (Ben).

The project roles

Project settings → Access explains them under What the roles mean: each role can do everything the role below it can, and more.

  • Project administrator: Everything in the project: objects, attributes and drawings, revisions, who has access, its settings, renaming and deleting it.
  • Lead engineer: Edits objects, attributes and drawings, issues revisions, approves branches and designs datasheets. Cannot change access or settings, or rename or delete the project.
  • Engineer: Creates and edits objects, attributes, relations and drawings, comments, and uses the co-engineer.
  • Reviewer: Views and comments. Changes nothing.
  • Viewer: Views and exports. Changes nothing.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1As Anna (Project administrator), select the project and click Members.A dialog Members of <project> with Members of the project (each with at most <role>), the form to admit someone, the list Roles in the project and the form Give a role.
2In Admit a person or group choose Ben, at most Engineer, role on the project Reviewer; click Admit.Ben is listed as Ben · at most Engineer, and the roles show Ben · Reviewer on project with Remove.
3Sign in as Ben, open the project and drag a symbol on a 2D space.The project opens, but the move is refused. The status bar shows Change refused with You may not do this here: it needs drawing.edit on … and No role you hold here includes drawing.edit.
3aAs Anna, choose Ben in Give a role and open Role.Project administrator and Lead engineer are greyed out: (above Ben's maximum: Engineer).
4As Anna, click Remove beside Ben's role.The role is gone from the list.

A role on the project counts as being a member at that level. An explicit maximum is needed to give someone more on a section, folder or drawing than on the whole project (for example Viewer on the project, Engineer on the Electrical folder, at most Engineer). Nobody who is not a member can be given a role inside the project. Until (optional) ends a role on a day you choose.

Groups are shown in the list as <name> (group). Only an administrator sees the whole organization in Person or group; others see only the people already on the project.

Without a passkey, a Project administrator acts as a Lead engineer and cannot give roles (see the passkey rule).

On E1 without a server with sign-in, Project settings → Access holds one line: Members and roles need E1 on a server with sign-in. On a server, someone who may not see the members reads the reason there instead.

Why a change is refused

AvailableSince 2026-11-03Updated 2026-11-09ORG-refusalsRoadmap M2.5, M2.9, 09.6sReview check J3

When you may not do something, E1 says so and names the permission that is missing, where it was needed, and whether your role or your license is the reason. Nothing is changed.

Where
The status bar (Change refused and the reason), a red line in a dialog, a read-only cell in a list (it shows the same reason), and the Chat tab
Who
Everyone

How to read a refusal

  • Missing role: You may not do this here: it needs <permission> on <scope>. No role you hold here includes <permission>. Ask the Project administrator for a role.
  • License too small: … Your <License> license does not include <permission>. Ask an administrator for another license.
  • Role waiting for a passkey: the text adds Your Owner role needs a passkey: add one under Account → Passkeys (your initial at the bottom of the left rail).
  • Trial ended: see Trials and the read-only lock.
  • Giving a role with more than you hold: You may not give <role> here: it includes <permissions>, which you do not hold here yourself. For an organization role: You may not give <role>: it includes <permissions>, which you do not hold yourself. Ask someone who holds them.
  • Your own role: You may not raise your own organization role: another Owner does that. or You may not give yourself an organization role: another Owner or Administrator does that.
  • A project you have no role in: You have no access to project <project>.
  • The last one: An organization always keeps at least one Owner. or A project always keeps at least one Project administrator. Give the role to someone else first.
  • Not a member: Roles are given to members of the organization; invite the person first.

Calling the API

  • Idempotency: send Idempotency-Key: a repeated call returns the first answer and changes nothing again (kept 24 hours).
  • Errors: 401 no valid token; 403 not allowed (the missing permission, or the token is read only or for other projects); 400 invalid input; 404 unknown; 409 changed meanwhile, or a key reused for another request; 422 a rule of the model; 429 too many calls (600 a minute per token).

A token never carries an Owner or Administrator role: administration is done signed in. Tokens and service accounts are never made with a token, and Alex never makes them. Every creation, revocation and first use from a new address is in the audit; every API call is in the API access log.

Records and other systems' ids

  • Read: GET /projects, /projects/{id}/objects?class=…, /objects/{id}?include=occurrences,relationships (the object with its rev, its values with units and comments, its catalog source and its ids in other systems), /objects/{id}/relationships, the project's saved queries: /projects/{id}/queries, and the rows of one: /projects/{id}/queries/{key}.
  • Queries: POST /projects/{id}/query with a query definition or a saved query's key gives its rows; POST /projects/{id}/queries/from-sentence with { "text": "all pumps without a motor" } gives the definition.
  • Change: PATCH /objects/{id} with If-Match: <rev> (a stale rev is 409, nothing changes). Relationships are read, not written.
  • By the other system's id: PUT /projects/{id}/objects/by-external/erp/10004711 creates the object the first time (201) and updates it after (200), so the other system never needs E1's ids. An object keeps one id per system, unique in the project; its page shows them in Details, on the row "In other systems".

Not in the API on purpose: drawing, deleting, relationships, the library and administration (use E1 itself), and mapping or scheduling (your script or middleware does that). Not built yet: address ranges in the token form (the API takes them).

Webhooks and the change feed

AvailableSince 2026-11-10ORG-webhooksRoadmap 19.3Review checks N3

Other systems keep up with E1 in two ways. A webhook tells a system at once: E1 sends each change to its address, signed, and tries again when it does not answer. The change feed lets a system ask: "what changed since I last looked?", with a cursor that never skips or repeats a change.

Where
File → Organization… → Webhooks; the change feed at /api/v1/projects/{id}/changes
Who
Owners and Administrators (the permission Manage connections) set up webhooks. Anyone who sees a project may read its change feed, with an access token.
Before you start
E1 with sign-in (the cloud). A receiver on the internet with an https:// address.

Set up a webhook

#ActionExpected result
1File → Organization… → Webhooks: type a name (for example "Maintenance system"), the receiver's address (https://…), tick the events it needs (Object created, Object updated, Object moved, Object deleted, Relationship created, …) and click Create webhook.The signing secret (whsec_…) is shown once, with Copy. The webhook is listed as Active. An address in a private network (10.…, 192.168.…, localhost) is refused.
2Change an object in a project, for example its flow rate.Within seconds the receiver gets a POST: {"subscription": …, "event": {"id", "type": "object.updated", "revision", "at", "project", "actor", "subject": {"id", "class", "name", "tag"}, "changed": {"attributes": ["plant.attr.flow-rate"]}}}. Events say what changed, never the values: the receiver reads the object with its own token.
3Click Deliveries… on its row.Each event with Delivered, Waiting (and the next try) or Failed, and every attempt with the answer and the time.
4Click Resend on a delivered or failed event.The event is sent again, with the same event id.
5Click Pause, change something, then Resume.Nothing is sent while paused; on resume the changes of the pause are sent.

For the receiver

  • Check the signature: the header E1-Signature: t=<unix seconds>,v1=<hex> is the HMAC-SHA256 of <t>.<body> with the signing secret. Refuse a request whose signature does not match or whose time is more than 5 minutes off.
  • Answer quickly with any 2xx. Anything else, or no answer within 10 seconds, is tried again after 1, 5 and 30 minutes, then 2, 5 and 10 hours; after 24 hours the delivery is marked failed (and noted in the audit).
  • At least once: an event may come twice (a retry, a resend). Ignore an E1-Event-Id you have already handled.

The change feed

  • GET /api/v1/projects/{id}/changes returns the oldest changes first, and a cursor. Keep the cursor and ask with ?since=<cursor> next time: you get only what changed after it, nothing twice, even after a restart. more: true means ask again at once.
  • Narrow it with types=object.created,object.updated and class=…; the cursor still moves past what is left out.
  • The feed keeps 90 days. An older cursor is refused (410 feed_expired) with the current revision: read the project in full, then follow the feed from there.

Not built yet: webhooks as part of a connection with its status page and failure notices, filters by attribute, and events of documents, branches and class upgrades.

Message queues: events to a broker, commands from one

AvailableSince 2026-11-12ORG-message-queuesRoadmap 19.5

Send the change feed's events to an MQTT, AMQP or Kafka broker instead of a web address, and let another system send E1 commands through a broker, each run as an access token.

Where
File → Organization… → Webhooks: the webhook form, and Queue consumers below it
Who
Owners and Administrators (Manage connections)
Before you start
A broker reachable over TLS on the internet (a customer's own installation may allow its own network), its user name and password, and for commands an access token (Organization → Access tokens) with the permissions the commands need

Events to a broker

#ActionExpected result
1In Webhooks, type a name and a broker address: mqtts://broker.example.com:8883/e1/events, amqps://broker.example.com/plant?exchange=e1&routingKey=e1.events (or ?queue=e1-events), or kafkas://k1.example.com:9093,k2.example.com:9093/e1-events.Broker user name and Broker password appear.
2Fill them in, tick the events and click Create webhook.The signing secret is shown once. The list shows the webhook with its transport (MQTT, AMQP, KAFKA) and address; the password is kept sealed and never shown.
3Change an object.The event is published to the topic, exchange or queue with the headers E1-Event-Id, E1-Event-Type, E1-Delivery-Id and E1-Signature (over MQTT, in the message's headers). Deliveries… shows it delivered once the broker confirmed it; a broker that cannot be reached is tried again, as a web address is.

Commands from a broker

#ActionExpected result
1In Queue consumers, give a Consumer name, the Broker address to read (an MQTT topic, an AMQP ?queue=, or a Kafka topic), the Access token, the broker's user name and password, and click Create queue consumer.It is listed, Active, with 0 run · 0 refused.
2From the other system, send { "id": "m-1", "tool": "set_values", "input": { "project": "…", "object": "…", "values": { … } }, "replyTo": "e1/replies" }.E1 runs it as the token and answers { "id": "m-1", "ok": true, "status": 200, "result": … } to replyTo (AMQP: the reply-to queue; Kafka: the reply-to header).
3Send the same message again.The same answer; nothing is changed twice. The same id with another command is answered with idempotency_conflict.
4Send a command the API does not offer, or a change with a read-only token.Answered with ok false and the reason; the list counts it as refused and shows the last error.

Only the API's commands run this way (finding and reading objects, setting values, renaming, external ids, queries, assistants), with the token's read-only and project limits and its rate. Addresses carry no user name or password: they are given apart.

Related: Webhooks and the change feed, Access tokens
