About this guide

In E1 wiring is data, not a drawing. A cabinet or junction box has a location and an enclosure; the enclosure holds rails, terminal strips and I/O cards; cables have their cores, and each core is landed on a terminal at both ends. From that data E1 traces every circuit from the field device to its I/O channel.

Typical workflow

  1. Make the locations and enclosures, with their rails, strips and cards.
  2. Add terminals to the strips, the channels and the instruments.
  3. Make the cables from the catalog's cable types, and land their cores on the terminals.
  4. Trace a circuit to check it reaches its channel, and set its construction status as the work goes on.
  5. Keep the cable lengths from 3D and by hand, and see where they differ; import the wiring of an existing plant or a vendor panel.
  6. Generate the loop diagrams from the wiring, issue them as a set, and build the wire list and other lists as queries.
  7. Give devices a wiring typical, open the Wiring page of a unit, let E1 propose where their signals land and accept it, and follow one signal's path.
  8. On the same page: the cable schedule and the cable editor, the enclosures and the strip designer, cable routes, potentials, the checks with the segregation matrix and wire numbers, and the lists.
  9. Check the entity and voltage budget; after issue, work with the wire move list and its field view, and compare with a revision.
  10. See each cabinet's fill and heat balance, exchange the I/O list with the control-system vendor, export markers, and make the terminal strip and cable block diagrams.

Before you start

You have a project whose library includes the Electrical foundation package (E1 library version 17 or newer: see Project settings). The examples use a marshalling cabinet A3 in equipment room CR1, a junction box JB-12, a transmitter TT-101, a field cable W-101 and a multicore W-201; on a project of your own, use your own names.

Cabinets and junction boxes: where they are and what they hold

AvailableSince 2026-11-11Updated 2026-11-13WIRE-enclosuresRoadmap 28.1, 28.7Review check R1

A cabinet or junction box is two objects. Its location (an equipment room or a field location) is in Areas; the enclosure (a junction box, marshalling cabinet, system cabinet, local panel or vendor panel) is in Assets and is located in it. The enclosure holds rails, terminal strips, I/O cards with their channels, interposing devices (barriers, isolators, relays), power supplies, earth bars and cable entries. An MCC and a distribution board are enclosures too.

Add terminals to a strip

Who
A license and a project role that may edit objects
Before you start

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click strip X1 of A3 → Wiring tools ▸ Add terminals….The window Add terminals to X1 opens with How many terminals (10 to start with): "They are numbered on from the last one. Add a first number after a space to count from it, for example 12 21."

Where
Right-click an enclosure, strip, channel, terminal, cable or core in the explorer → Wiring tools ▸ Trace circuit
Who
Anyone who may view the project
Before you start
The cores are landed (Cables and their cores).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Right-click field cable W-101 → Wiring tools ▸ Trace circuit.E1 shows the way, for example "TT-101 → JB-12 → A3", and where it ends: "Ends at +CR1-A3-A5-1:1 (channel), +CR1-A3-A5-1:2 (channel)."

Cable lengths and the wiring settings

AvailableSince 2026-11-11Updated 2026-11-13WIRE-cable-lengthsRoadmap 28.1, 28.7Review check R3

A cable has a Length (by hand) and a Length from 3D (written by the 3D model's script through E1's API). E1 fills Length used itself: the length by hand when there is one, else the one from 3D. When the two differ by more than the project's tolerance, that is a finding.

Where
A cable's Attributes tab, group Line; Configuration → Project settings → Wiring (also File → Project settings…)
Who
Lengths: a license and a project role that may edit objects. Settings: a project role that may configure the project.
Before you start
A cable exists.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1On W-201's Attributes tab, type 120 m in Length from 3D.Length used shows 120 m.
2Type 100 m in Length (by hand).Length used shows 100 m: the length by hand wins.
3Try to type in Length used.Refused: "The length used is calculated from the length by hand and the length from 3D; set one of those."
4Open Configuration → Project settings and go to the Wiring card.Cable length tolerance is 10 %, Name printed first is Instrument tag (TT-101), and Print the other name beside it is ticked.
5Type 25 in Cable length tolerance and leave the box.Saved at once. With 100 m by hand and 120 m from 3D the lengths now agree; at 10 % they did not.

Lengths that differ are listed on the Checks tab of the Wiring page under Cable lengths differ, with conductor cores landed at one end only, a terminal carrying the cores of two cables, and a strip longer than its rail. A cable routed through route segments also has a routed length, the sum of its segments' lengths (Cable routes): it is shown beside the length by hand and the length from 3D, never written over them. The cable schedule's Length says where its length came from: nothing by hand, 3D or route.

Cabinet fill and heat balance

AvailableSince 2026-11-11Updated 2026-11-13WIRE-fill-heatRoadmap 28.5, 28.7Review check R7

A cabinet's or junction box's window has a Fill and heat page with its fill: each strip as a bar of used, spare and free terminals, each card with its channels used by type. Below it, the cabinet's heat balance from physics: the heat its devices give off at their load (a card for the channels in use, a supply, a barrier, plus heat added by hand) against the heat its free walls, a fan and a cooling unit take away. The result is the inside temperature, the temperature at the top with the project's allowance, the margin to the cabinet's limit, and the cooling still missing.

Where
Open an enclosure (a cabinet, junction box, panel or MCC) and click Fill and heat in the tabs at the top of its window, or Fill and heat ↗ in the enclosure on its Wiring page; its Attributes tab, groups Size and Heat
Who
Anyone who may view the project; the values need a license and a role that may edit objects
Before you start
The enclosure has its width, height and depth, its Heat transfer coefficient (from the maker's data, about 5.5 W/(m²·K) for sheet steel), Ambient temperature and Heat limit; its devices have their heat loss (catalog items carry it).

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1On a 600 × 2000 × 600 mm system cabinet S1, type back, left, bottom in Covered faces, 100 m³/h in Fan airflow, 50 W in Heat added by hand, a limit of 40 °C and an ambient of 35 °C.Each value is saved. The covered faces give no heat away: the free walls are the top, the front and the right, 2.76 m².
2Click Fill and heat in the tabs at the top of the window."36.7 °C inside, 41.7 °C at the top, 1.7 K above the limit; 83.8 W of cooling missing." Below it, how: the heat in, the walls and coefficient, the fan, and the devices that give the most heat.
3Add a power supply without a Heat loss.A finding: "S1: the heat loss of power supply G2 is not known; the heat balance does not count it."
4Type 100 W in Cooling power.The cabinet stays at the ambient temperature.

This is a heat balance from physics with every input shown; it is not a temperature-rise verification under IEC 61439 or IEC TR 60890, which a contract may still ask for. The top allowance is 5 K unless the project sets another.

Fieldbus segments

AvailableSince 2026-11-11Updated 2026-11-13WIRE-fieldbusRoadmap 28.6, 28.7Review check R8

Signals and wiring typicals

AvailableSince 2026-11-11Updated 2026-11-13WIRE-typicalsRoadmap 28.3, 28.7Review check R5

Most devices of a plant follow a handful of patterns: a 2-wire transmitter, a solenoid valve, a motor-operated valve. A wiring typical describes one pattern once: the device's signals (open command, closed limit, position …), the field cables and which pair each signal takes, options such as Position feedback, variants (named sets of option values) and the naming of cables and wires. Given to a device, it makes the device's I/O signals, its terminals, and its field cables with their cores landed on the device and numbered.

Entity check and voltage budget

AvailableSince 2026-11-11Updated 2026-11-13WIRE-circuit-checksRoadmap 28.4, 28.7Review check R6

For an intrinsically safe loop E1 compares the barrier's Uo, Io and Po with the device's Ui, Ii and Pi, and its Co and Lo with the device's Ci and Li plus the cable's capacitance and inductance for its length. For a loop with a supply it works out the voltage left at the device at 20 mA: the supply, less the barrier's drop, the cable's loop resistance and the channel's input resistance. A value it needs and does not have is reported as missing, never taken as zero.
