About this guide

This guide shows you around the E1 window: the menus, the left rail and its places, the explorer, the tabs, the side panel, the status bar, the keyboard shortcuts, your own settings and Help. Read it first. The other guides assume you know where these things are.

The usual order

  1. Learn the window layout and the left rail.
  2. Look through the three menus: File, Edit and Help. Everything else is where you work: on the drawing toolbar, in the right-click menus and on the buttons of each page.
  3. Work with tabs and the path bar, floating windows and hiding and showing panels.
  4. Read the status bar and choose your appearance.
  5. Learn the keyboard shortcuts.
  6. Open the places (Inbox, Configuration, Settings, Account) and set your Settings.
  7. See what is still being built in Under construction.

Before you start

E1 is open in the browser or the desktop app, and you are signed in where your installation asks for it. See Sign in to E1. The pictures in this guide show the demo project Demo Plant of the organization Demo Company in the dark appearance.

The E1 window

AvailableUpdated 2026-11-09GS-layoutReview check A1

The E1 window has seven parts: the menu bar, the left rail, the explorer, the tab strip, the editor area, the Alex side panel, and the status bar. Everything you do starts in one of them.

Where
The whole window, as soon as E1 has opened
Who
Everyone who can open E1
Before you start
E1 is open and you are signed in where your installation asks for it.

The parts of the window

  1. Menu bar. The E1 logo and the word E1, then the menus File, Edit and Help.
  2. Left rail. Icons only: the Inbox bell, the Explorer and Alex switches, Configuration, Settings and your account.
  3. Explorer. The tree of your projects. Its header shows the name of your organization and the explorer's buttons.
  4. Tab strip. One tab for each open 2D space, list, object, place or page.
  5. Editor area. The path bar and the content of the active tab, for example a drawing with its toolbar.
  6. Side panel. Alex, the one assistant, with one row of tabs: Co-engineer (its findings, local), Ask, Agent and Plan (a conversation with a language model) and E1 Guide.
  7. Status bar. Whether your changes are saved, what is selected, the units, the appearance and the store E1 runs on.

You can drag the splitters between the explorer and the editor, and between the editor and the side panel, to change their widths. The explorer is 180 to 480 pixels wide (260 at first), the side panel 280 to 560 pixels (340 at first). E1 keeps these widths in this browser.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Open E1.The status bar shows Saved, the menu bar reads E1, and the explorer tree is visible.
2Look at the explorer header.It shows a button named after your organization (here Demo Company). There is no Workspace row in the tree.
3Close every tab with its ×.The editor shows No tab open and "Select a project or section in the explorer to open it here."
4Drag the splitter to the right of the explorer to the right.The explorer gets wider, up to its limit. After a reload it keeps its width.
5Click File, then point at Edit without clicking.The Edit menu opens in place of the File menu: while a menu is open, pointing at another menu name opens it.
6Press Esc (or click outside the menu).The menu closes.

Review check A1 is out of date. It expects a Workspace row and a Lines section in the explorer. Since 2026-11-07 the explorer header names the organization and there is no Workspace row; Lines is a group under Assets, not a section.

ItemShortcutWhat it does

Tabs and the path bar

AvailableUpdated 2026-11-12GS-tabs

Everything you open (a 2D space, an object, a list, a place) gets a tab. Under the tabs the path bar shows where the open item lives, from the organization down. E1 remembers your tabs for you.

Where
The tab strip above the editor, and the path bar under it
Who
Everyone
Before you start
A project is in the explorer.

How tabs work

  • Each tab has an icon, a name, Detach (to a floating window) and Close. Hover a tab to see its full path, for example Demo Company / Demo Plant / Model Space / Assets.
  • Middle-click a tab to close it. Turn the mouse wheel over the strip to scroll it sideways.
  • Right-click a project in the explorer → Close all tabs closes every tab of that project and leaves the others open; greyed out when none is open. A project moved to the Bin closes its tabs too.
  • With many tabs, a button All open tabs (N) appears at the end of the strip. It lists every tab, with a search box Search tabs; ↑ and ↓ move through the list.
  • Dragging a tab floats it only once the pointer leaves the tab strip; a short drag inside the strip does nothing. Tabs cannot be dragged to a new order.
  • The path bar shows crumbs separated by /. The first one is the organization. Click a crumb to select that item. Places and Under construction have no path bar.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Double-click a 2D space in the explorer.A tab opens with the drawing; the path bar shows the organization, the project, Model Space and the 2D space.
2Click × (Close) on that tab.The tab is gone, with its drawing.
3Double-click the 2D space again.The tab is back, active, with the drawing.
4Double-click ten or more objects under Assets.The tabs shrink and All open tabs (N) appears at the end of the strip.
5Click All open tabs (N), type V5 in Search tabs and press Enter.The list shows one entry; after Enter the V5 tab is active.
6Click the project's crumb in the path bar.The project is selected in the explorer.
7Reload the page.The same tabs are open and the same tab is active.

Floating windows

AvailableUpdated 2026-11-09GS-floating-windowsRoadmap M0

Take a tab out of the main window to see two things side by side, for example a drawing on a second screen. Changes you make in a floating window are saved like any other.

Where
A tab's Detach button, or drag a tab out of the tab strip; the same for a chat tab in Alex’s conversation
Who
Everyone
Before you start
In the browser, allow pop-ups for E1.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1On the Assets 2D space tab, click Detach (the box with an arrow).A new window opens with the drawing. Its title bar shows the tab's name and an Attach button.
2In the floating window, place a pump with Object → Pump → Add to diagram and click on the 2D space.The pump appears in both windows; the main window's status bar shows Saved.

4Click Attach.The floating window closes and the tab is back in the main window.

The chat tabs in Alex’s conversation float the same way: click their Detach, or drag them out of the chat tab bar. In the desktop app a floating window is a real window of its own, and E1 opens it again where it was at the next start. When the item in a floating window is deleted, it shows "This tab is closed" and "The item is no longer in the project."

The status bar

AvailableGS-status-bar

The strip along the bottom tells you whether your changes are saved, what is selected, what your last edit touched, and which store E1 runs on. It also holds the units and appearance chips.

Where
The bottom edge of the window
Who
Everyone
Before you start
E1 is open.

From left to right

  1. Save state: Loading, Saving…, Saved, Save failed, Changed elsewhere — reloaded or Change refused. A failure is red, and its reason is shown next to it and on hover.
  2. Name of the selected item.
  3. Impact after an edit on the drawing, for example "Connected: P1, T1 and 3 lines". Hover it to see each symbol and line. It stays until your next edit.
  4. Unit system of the project (MET, Imperial, SI, a company system, or Units when none is chosen); a click opens Project settings. See Units of measurement.
  5. Appearance chip Dark or Light.
  6. Store: where your projects are kept. See Which store E1 runs on.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Rename a project.The save state briefly reads Saving…, then Saved.
2Move a pump on the drawing that has lines connected.The impact reads "Connected: …" with the connected symbols and lines.
3As a Reviewer on a project, try to move a symbol.The save state reads Change refused in red, with the reason, for example "You may not do this here: it needs drawing.edit on …".

Usage statistics: your choice

AvailableSince 2026-11-09GS-usage-statistics

E1 asks you once whether it may share usage statistics with LANXTOR. It is off unless you agree. With it on, E1 counts per day how long you actively work (only while you click or type), how many clicks and key presses, and which functions and places you use. It never records what you draw, type or name. LANXTOR uses it to see which parts of E1 help, and which need work.

Where
The question after you sign in; later: left rail → gear Settings → card Usage statistics
Who
Everyone with sign-in, each for themselves

Steps

#ActionExpected result

3Open Settings.The card Usage statistics shows your answer: Share or Do not share.
4Click Do not share.Nothing is shared. What was shared before was deleted.

Each person answers for themselves; your organization's administrators cannot answer for you. A window left open without input adds no active time. See the privacy policy.

Units of measurement

AvailableSince 2026-11-09GS-unitsRoadmap 01.2bReview check L26

The status bar shows the unit system of the project you are working in: MET, Imperial, SI, or a company system. A click opens Project settings, where it is chosen.

Where
Status bar → the unit system chip
Who
Everyone; choosing the system needs the Project administrator role
Before you start
Something in a project is selected.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Select something in a project.The chip reads the project's unit system, or Units when none is chosen yet.
2Click the chip.Project settings opens; its Units card sets the system.
