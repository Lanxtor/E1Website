About this guide

This guide covers the 2D space view, where you draw P&IDs. Every symbol on a 2D space is an object of the project, and every line you draw is a pipe, cable, wire or signal in the model, so the drawing, the explorer and the lists always show the same plant.

The typical workflow

  1. Open a 2D space and find your way around it: the 2D space view, the drawing toolbar and Snap, Grid and Ortho.
  2. Place equipment: place a symbol, or drop an object from the explorer. Other kinds of objects come from the Object button.
  3. Connect it: draw pipes, signals and cables with Connector, following the connection rules.
  4. Arrange it: select, move, flip, resize and align, copy and paste, delete.
  5. Annotate it: tags and line numbers, text notes, line styles, flow arrows and crossings.
  6. Organize and check it: layers, system and area frames, off-page connectors, Highlight flow, chat messages pinned on the 2D space.
  7. Let E1 help: predictive mode, engineering patterns and generated loop diagrams.

Printing a 2D space and its PDF are described in the documents guide. The end of this page lists what is planned for drawing: the symbol designer, piping (pipelines, pipe classes, the line list, DEXPI), CAD import and redlining.

Before you start

  • You are signed in and have a project open (create a project).
  • To change a drawing you need an Editor license and a project role that may edit drawings. With read access you can open, zoom, pan, select and highlight, but not change anything.
  • The examples use the demo project: the 2D space Assets with the pumps Pump1 to Pump3 and the valves V1 to V10. On your own project, use your own names.

Open a 2D space, zoom and pan

AvailableSince 2026-11-04Updated 2026-11-09DRAW-sheet-viewRoadmap M3.2, M3.11Review check B1

A 2D space opens in its own tab as a drawing. You zoom with the mouse wheel or the zoom controls, and pan with a drag, the Pan tool or the Space bar. Symbols and text stay sharp at every zoom.

Where
Double-click a 2D space in the explorer, or click its tab. Zoom: the zoom controls at the right end of the status bar under the 2D space, the mouse wheel and the keys.
Who
Anyone who can open the project
Before you start
The project has a 2D space, for example Assets.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Double-click the 2D space Assets in the explorer.The 2D space opens in a tab. The drawing toolbar is above it, and the status bar below it shows Select, the toggles Snap, Grid, Ortho and the zoom controls.
2Point at a symbol and turn the mouse wheel.The view zooms in or out. The point under the pointer stays where it is.
3Click Fit drawing in the status bar (or press Ctrl+0 (⌘0)).The whole drawing is shown.
4Click the zoom percentage in the status bar (Zoom) and choose 200%.The menu offers Zoom in, Zoom out, 50%, 75%, 100%, 150%, 200%, 400%, Fit drawing and Zoom to selection. After the choice the button reads 200%.
5Click Zoom in or Zoom out in the status bar, or press Ctrl++ (⌘+) or Ctrl+− (⌘−).The zoom steps up by a quarter or down by a fifth. At the closest or farthest zoom the button is greyed out.
6Select a symbol, then click the zoom percentage and choose Zoom to selection.The view zooms to the selected symbol.
7Drag with the middle mouse button, or with the right button held down.The drawing pans, whatever tool is active. A right-click without moving opens a menu instead.
8Hold Shift and turn the mouse wheel.The drawing pans sideways.
9Click Pan on the toolbar (or press H), then drag with the left button.The status bar reads Pan, and a left-drag pans. Press V or click Select to go back to selecting.
10With Select active, hold Space and drag.The drawing pans while Space is held.

The space name at the right end of the toolbar's first row (for example Assets) names the space this 2D space draws.

Place a symbol

Each symbol you place is a new object in the project with its next free name or tag. Two clicks set its inlet and outlet, so you choose its direction and size as you place it.

Where
Drawing toolbar → Object while Object type shows Asset, or Object type → Asset
Who
Editor license and a project role that may edit drawings
Before you start
A 2D space is open.

The Add asset window

  • Search symbols finds symbols by name, type, standard, key, class or diagram type.
  • The filter menus Standard and Diagram have check boxes with counts and a Show all button. Clear filters resets them. On a 2D space with a diagram type, the Diagram filter starts on that type.
  • On the left, All with a count and the class tree.
  • The Name field is filled with the next free name.
  • The symbol grid, grouped by kind; on All, a Recent group comes first.
  • The details of the chosen symbol, with Diagram types check boxes. Ticking one sets that diagram type for the shape across the project. With nothing chosen it says "Select a symbol to see its details."
  • Cancel and Add to diagram. A double-click on a symbol adds it at once; the arrow keys move the choice.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Click Object on the toolbar (its type shows Asset).The Add asset window opens. Esc would close it.
2Type "centrifugal" in Search symbols.The grid shows only the matching symbols. A search with no match shows "No symbols match "…"."
3Click Centrifugal pump, type the name P-NEW in Name, then click Add to diagram.The window closes. The status bar reads Place asset with the hint "Click to set the inlet, then the outlet. Placing repeats for the next one; right-click or Esc stops. F flips the symbol."
4Click once on an empty spot of the 2D space.The inlet is set, and a ghost of the pump follows the pointer. A first or second click near the end point of a pipe or a nozzle takes that point, so the symbol connects there.
5Move the pointer straight down and click.A vertical pump P-NEW is placed. A short second click keeps the default size; a longer one sets the size, within limits.
6Click elsewhere, press F, then click a second time.A second pump is placed, facing the other way, with the next free name.
7Click once on an empty spot of the 2D space, then press Esc.The first click is taken back; the tool still places.
8Press Esc again (or right-click).Placing stops; no menu opens. The status bar reads Select. With no first click, one Esc stops placing.
9Open the window again, choose Gate valve, click Add to diagram, then click once on the middle of an existing pipe.The valve sits in the line along it, and the pipe is split there.
10Look at the explorer under Assets.The new pumps and the valve are there as objects. Pumps whose class has a tag rule get tags such as P-001, P-002.

The tags come from your project's tag rules (Tag rules). To place an object that already exists, drag it from the explorer (Drop from the explorer).

Drawing toolbar → Connector; the kind in Connector type.
Who
Editor license and a project role that may edit drawings
Before you start
A 2D space with at least two symbols is open.

Steps

#ActionExpected result

2Choose Flow lines, then click Connector.The button is highlighted. The status bar reads Connector · Flow lines with the hint "Click point by point to draw; each segment stays horizontal or vertical. Connection rules: one pipe per nozzle, lines that follow and heal

AvailableSince 2026-11-04Updated 2026-11-09DRAW-connection-rulesRoadmap M3.1Review checks B4, A3

E1 keeps the drawing a correct model. A nozzle takes one pipe end, connected lines follow when you move a symbol, and deleting a symbol that sits in a line joins the line again.

Where
On any 2D space, while you draw, move and delete
Who
Editor license and a project role that may edit drawings
Before you start
A 2D space with a pump, a valve and a pipe between them.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Drag the pump to a new place.The pipe follows and stays connected, its segments horizontal or vertical.
2With Connector on Flow lines, draw a second pipe to the nozzle that already has the first pipe.It is refused with a message of the form "The nozzle … already ends …; … cannot end there too", and nothing is drawn.
3Draw a Wire or Logic line to that same nozzle.It is allowed: signals may still end there.
4Place a valve into the middle of a pipe, then select the valve and delete it (Delete the object).The pipe is continuous again across the gap. A branch that ended on the valve goes with it.

Undo, redo, copy, paste and duplicate

AvailableSince 2026-11-04Updated 2026-11-11DRAW-clipboardRoadmap M3.4Review checks A4, B5

Copy, paste and duplicate make new objects with new tags; cut removes them to the Deleted bin. Undo and redo work step by step across the drawing, the explorer and attributes, one history per person per project.

Where
Drawing toolbar (Undo, Redo, Copy, Paste, Duplicate), the Edit menu, and the right-click menus
Who
Editor license and a project role that may edit drawings
Before you start
A 2D space with symbols is open.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1Move a symbol, then press Ctrl+Z (⌘Z).The move is undone.
2Press Ctrl+Shift+Z (⇧⌘Z) or Ctrl+Y.The move is done again.
3Select a pump and press Ctrl+C (⌘C), then Ctrl+V (⌘V).A copy of the pump appears. It is a new object with a new tag, shown in the explorer, with the pump's values and its parts.
4Select a valve and press Ctrl+D (⌘D) or click Duplicate.A duplicate with a new name appears beside it.
5Select the copy and press Ctrl+X (⌘X).It leaves the 2D space and goes to the Deleted bin.
6Press Ctrl+Z (⌘Z) three times.The last three steps are undone one by one.

A copy takes its original's design values (calculated values come from their rules again), the relationships between the objects you copied, and the parts of an object that are not drawn. Copy every symbol of a placed module and the copies are a new instance of it: A placed module on its objects. The values come in a second step after the copy is made: Ctrl+Z takes them back first.

Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+D and Ctrl+A (⌘ on a Mac) act on the 2D space only when the 2D space was the last pane you clicked. After a click in the explorer, click the 2D space first. Paste in the right-click menu is greyed out until you have copied something. To undo a change of someone else's, or an older change, use History (History and undo).

Configure modules from what a unit needs

AvailableSince 2026-11-12Updated 2026-11-12DRAW-module-configureRoadmap 32.6Review check M15

Say what a unit needs in words; E1 proposes the modules, their variants and how they join, and arranges them on the 2D space as one change.

Where
On a 2D space: Add asset → Modules → Configure…
Who
Engineer and above
Before you start
Published modules, for example Block valve and Reducer station, each with a process inlet and outlet as tie-in points.

Steps

#ActionExpected result
1In What the unit needs type a block valve, then a reducer and click Propose.The two modules, each with the words it is for, and the join Block valve Outlet → Reducer station Inlet.
2Choose a Variant where a module has variants, or Leave out one.The proposal changes; nothing is placed yet.
3Click Arrange.The modules are placed left to right in flow order, each as its variant, and a pipe joins each pair. One Undo takes all of it back.

A part of the sentence no module matches is listed. A variant or option the sentence names (with standby) is chosen. The pipe runs square from the one port to the other; a tie-in point at a line end is joined by hand. Alex proposes with propose_module_arrangement and arranges with arrange_modules, choosing modules, variants and joins itself; in Review first the arrangement is a proposal you apply.
