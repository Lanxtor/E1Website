Where your data is

The E1 Cloud runs on Microsoft Azure in the West Europe region (the Netherlands). Backups are kept in a second EU region, North Europe (Ireland). Your data does not leave the EU for storage or backups.

  • Database: Azure Database for PostgreSQL, on a private network with no public address. Only E1's own server can connect to it.
  • Files (documents, drawings, photos): Azure Blob Storage, reached only with E1's managed identity. Shared keys are switched off.
  • Secrets (database passwords, keys): Azure Key Vault, never in code or configuration files.

If your company must keep its data in its own environment, E1 can run in your own Microsoft Azure subscription instead (below).

Separated from other customers

Every organization's records carry its organization, and the database itself enforces the separation with row-level security: E1's server connects with a role that cannot read past it. Every request from your people runs inside your organization, so a mistake in one part of the software cannot show them another customer's data. The separation is tested automatically on every change to E1.

Encryption

  • In transit: HTTPS (TLS 1.2 or newer) between your browser or the E1 app and the E1 Cloud, and TLS with certificate verification between E1's server and its database.
  • At rest: the database, its backups, the files and the logs are encrypted by Azure with AES-256.
  • Your own key: if your company must hold its own encryption keys, run E1 in your own Azure, where your IT can use customer-managed keys from your own Key Vault.

Protection against deletion

The database server, the file storage and the key vault carry Azure delete locks, so they cannot be deleted by mistake. E1's operators are alerted at once if any of them, or a lock, is deleted. Only two people at LANXTOR are owners of the E1 Cloud's Azure subscription, both with multi-factor sign-in.

Restores are tested

A backup only counts if it can be restored. Every change to E1 is tested by restoring a backup into an empty database and comparing it with the original. At least every quarter, E1's operators restore the whole E1 Cloud database onto a separate server, check it, and record how long it took against the 8-hour target. The results are available to customers on request.

If your organization needs something back

Most mistakes can be undone in E1 by your own administrators: every object keeps its history, deleted items go to the project's Deleted bin first, and documents keep their revisions.

For anything older or larger, contact support@lanxtor.com with the time it was last right. Because many companies share the E1 Cloud, E1 does not rewind the whole service for one of them. It restores a copy from that moment on a separate server and takes out only your organization's data, so nobody else loses work. You then decide with E1 what is put back.

Your own copy, any time

Your organization's administrators can export all of its data whenever they like (Organization… → Export → Export everything…). The archive holds every project's records, history, comments, documents and files in a documented format, so your company can keep its own backups in its own systems too.

Deleted data and backups

When you delete data, or your organization leaves E1 and asks for its data to be erased, it is removed from the live service at once. Backups are not edited. Deleted data therefore remains in backups until they expire: 35 days for the database and 30 days for files. If a restore ever brings deleted data back, it is deleted again.

In your own Microsoft Azure

E1 can be installed in your company's own Azure subscription instead of the E1 Cloud: the database, the files, the sign-in and the server are all in your account, in the region you choose, on your Azure bill. Your people sign in with their company accounts (Microsoft Entra ID). Nothing of yours is stored at LANXTOR.

How updates reach your installation

LANXTOR publishes E1 as numbered releases with release notes. An update copies the new release into your own container registry and switches your server to it; E1 brings its database up to date as it starts, and your data stays where it is. If the new release does not start, the update switches back to the one before by itself. Each release's notes say whether it changes the database, so you know before you update whether going back would also need a restore of the database to the moment before the update.
